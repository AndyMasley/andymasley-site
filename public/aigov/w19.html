Phase IV · authored

Training data

Read

  • Dolma: an Open Corpus of Three Trillion Tokens for Language Model Pretraining Research (Soldaini et al., Allen Institute for AI, ACL 2024, arXiv:2402.00159) — the source inventory, the pipeline stage order, the content filters, and the open toolkit. Redraw the pipeline closed-book, one sentence per stage on why it sits where it does.
  • The FineWeb Datasets: Decanting the Web for the Finest Text Data at Scale (Penedo et al., Hugging Face, arXiv:2406.17557) — "Building FineWeb" (ablations, filter stack, MinHash dedup) and "FineWeb-Edu" (the rubric, the classifier, the threshold). Grade the claim "more deduplication is better" against their own ablation.
  • Cosmopedia: how to create large-scale synthetic data for pre-training Large Language Models (Ben Allal et al., Hugging Face, March 2024, huggingface.co/blog/cosmopedia) — prompt curation and the benchmark decontamination protocol behind 25B Mixtral-generated tokens. Write the evidence brief on "synthetic data sidesteps the consent problem."
  • DataComp-LM: In search of the next generation of training sets for language models (Li, Fang, Smyrnis, Ivgi et al., arXiv:2406.11794) — optional. A controlled curation benchmark: fixed data pool, fixed training recipes, model-based filtering the decisive knob. Capability-side evidence only; nothing on consent, provenance, or deletion.
  • CS336 Lectures 13 and 14, "Data" (cs336.stanford.edu) — optional. Sources and datasets, then filtering, deduplication, mixing, and synthetic data; Assignment 4 turns raw Common Crawl into pretraining data. Watch after the papers.

Do

Stream a few thousand documents from the FineWeb dataset on Hugging Face (streaming, so you never download 15T of anything). Implement two heuristics from this week's papers, a Gopher-style repetition rule and a C4-style terminal-punctuation rule, plus exact paragraph dedup. Predict each filter's removal rate before running it, then inspect ten removed documents and find at least one false positive.

Audit yourself for contamination. nanoGPT's prepare.py split your W15 Shakespeare corpus by position: first 90% train, last 10% val, no overlap check across the boundary. Take every 8-word n-gram from the val split and count verbatim hits in train. Predict the count first, then keep the number.

Then write a two-page data-governance plan for a hypothetical pretraining run, in the form you would hand an auditor. Six sections: sources, with the provenance record each document carries (origin URL, crawl date, license or opt-out status); the filter stack, each filter paired with the ablation that justifies it; dedup level and scope, with a position on FineWeb's global-dedup trap; PII handling; the eval-decontamination protocol (which eval sets, what n-gram size, what happens on a hit); and the documentation the pipeline emits, mapped to Annex XI's data fields and the Article 53(1)(d) public summary.

Done when the plan names one concrete contamination channel your own W15 Shakespeare run had, with the overlap count your build measured, and points to the specific check in your protocol that would catch that same channel in a frontier run.

Skip if you can already take a public dataset card (Dolma's works), name the pipeline stage behind each documented choice, the Annex XI field it would populate, and one thing the card leaves unauditable.

Take away

  • "Data quality" has only an operational definition: a filter is good if models trained on the filtered corpus score better. A serious corpus is therefore a stack of ablation results, one experiment per stage.
  • The filter is increasingly a model. FineWeb-Edu's classifier and Cosmopedia's generator put one model's judgment upstream of the next model's corpus, and those judgments become corpus law that no human wrote or can read off.
  • Dedup intensity is not monotone in quality. Global MinHash across all 96 Common Crawl snapshots removed up to ~90% of the oldest snapshots, and the surviving 10% was worse than what was removed, so FineWeb dedups per snapshot.
  • Provenance is not a copyright field. Without a per-document record of where text came from you cannot trace an eval-set leak, honor a deletion request or a machine-readable opt-out, excise a poisoned source, or audit representation. It is captured at collection or it is gone.
  • The decision this bears on is what pretraining data documentation to require. EU AI Act Annex XI Section 1 point 2(c), implementing Article 53(1)(a), obliges GPAI providers to document the "type and provenance of data and curation methodologies," data-point counts, how data was obtained and selected, and measures to detect unsuitable sources and identifiable biases.
  • Article 53(1)(d) adds a public "sufficiently detailed summary" of training content, per the AI Office template published 24 July 2025. Counts and source categories are cheap to produce after the fact; provenance cannot be retrofitted, and a requirement written without that distinction buys either theater or non-compliance.
  • Article 53(1)(c)'s duty to honor machine-readable text-and-data-mining reservations under Article 4(3) of Directive (EU) 2019/790 is the live fight over whether scraping for training needs permission at all. R5 takes it up.
Check yourself
  1. Name three engineering operations (not legal duties) that break when the pipeline keeps no per-document provenance.
  2. FineWeb deduplicates each Common Crawl snapshot independently. What did the global-dedup ablation show, why, and what does that do to "deduplicate harder" as a universal recipe?
  3. FineWeb-Edu's filter is a classifier. What new dependency does that insert into the corpus, and what five things should an auditor now demand to see?
  4. Your W15 run: name its contamination channel precisely, and the frontier-scale check that would catch the same channel.

Answers. (1) Any three of: tracing an eval-set leak back to its source; executing a deletion or opt-out, since you cannot remove what you cannot locate and honoring a TDM reservation requires the origin URL; excising or reweighting a source discovered to be poisoned or low-quality; choosing dedup scope per source; auditing representation across sources. (2) Global MinHash across all 96 snapshots removed up to ~90% of the oldest crawls, and the surviving 10% was worse than what was removed (more ads, keyword lists, malformed text), because dedup eliminated large clusters of stable, often good content while small scattered clusters of junk survived. Per-snapshot dedup trained better models, so dedup intensity is a knob to ablate rather than maximize. (3) The corpus now depends on Llama-3-70B-Instruct's judgment of "educational," distilled into a classifier. The auditor asks for the scoring rubric and prompt, the annotation sample, the classifier itself, the threshold (3), and the validation figures (F1 82%); without those, "filtered for quality" is unfalsifiable. (4) Position-based splitting with no overlap check: prepare.py takes the first 90% of one document as train and the last 10% as val, and Shakespeare repeats strings verbatim across that boundary (speaker headers, stock phrases), so validation loss partly scores memorized strings; your build counted the hits. The frontier check is train-eval decontamination run before training, n-gram overlap between corpus and every eval set (Cosmopedia's version: 10-gram overlap then sequence matching at 0.5), hits logged and removed.
