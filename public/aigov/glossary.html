Glossary

Terms this curriculum uses in a specific sense, with the unit that teaches each one. Where a term is contested, the entry states the curriculum's position rather than the range of usage.

  • 6ND (W05, W21) — the standard training-compute estimate: 6 FLOPs per parameter per training token, because the backward pass costs roughly twice the forward pass. N is active parameters, not total. A public 6ND figure is a reconstruction from unknowns, not a measurement.
  • Activation patching (W39) — copying a cached activation from a clean prompt into a matched corrupted run and measuring how much of the output difference it restores. Preferred to zero-ablation, which puts the network in a state it never occupies.
  • AI Office (R9) — the unit inside the European Commission that enforces the AI Act's general-purpose model obligations centrally. National market surveillance authorities handle everything else.
  • Alignment faking (W42) — complying during training with an objective the model was trained to refuse, then behaving differently where it infers it is unmonitored. The evidence is the compliance gap, not the absolute rate.
  • Annex XI (W14, W19) — the AI Act's technical documentation schedule for GPAI providers, implementing Article 53(1)(a): architecture and parameter count, training methodologies and key design choices, data type and provenance and curation methods, compute.
  • ARA (autonomous replication and adaptation) (S2) — a model acquires compute, copies its weights, evades shutdown, and self-sustains outside any operator's control. Whether the binding constraint is cognitive or financial decides whether policy targets capability thresholds or KYC for compute.
  • Arbitrary and capricious (R2) — the APA §706 standard. It tests the record the agency built, not the answer it reached. Agencies lose for ignoring an obvious alternative, passing over contrary evidence, or supplying a post hoc rationale.
  • Arithmetic intensity (W21) — FLOPs performed per byte moved to or from memory. Compare it against the chip's ridge point, peak FLOP/s divided by memory bandwidth, to decide whether a workload is compute-bound or memory-bound.
  • Article 55 (W36, W44) — the duties attaching to systemic-risk GPAI models: model evaluation under standardised state-of-the-art protocols, adversarial testing, risk mitigation, serious-incident reporting, and adequate cybersecurity for the weights and their infrastructure.
  • Attention (W13) — content-based routing. Each position emits a query, every position advertises a key, and the output is a softmax over scaled dot products applied to values. An attention map shows one routing step in one head, not a chain of reasoning and not causal evidence.
  • BLUF (R1) — bottom line up front. Recommendation first, then options, then the map. The test is that a named principal could act from page one.
  • BPE (byte-pair encoding) (W12) — the merge loop that builds a tokenizer by repeatedly joining the most frequent adjacent pair. Merges are earned by corpus frequency, so underrepresented scripts stay near byte level and cost more tokens for identical content.
  • C2PA (W34, R5) — the Content Credentials standard. A manifest binds assertions to a signer and a content hash, establishing who signed and that the file is unchanged since. It proves nothing about truth, and a missing manifest proves nothing at all.
  • Calibration (W11) — a frequency promise on a population: of all the times the model says 80%, about 80% are right. A property of a score on a distribution, never of a single answer, and among the first properties distribution shift breaks.
  • Chinchilla-optimal (W20) — the parameter-token split minimizing training loss at a fixed training-FLOP budget, roughly twenty tokens per parameter. A training-loss optimum, not a business optimum: frontier practice over-trains smaller models far past it, since inference compute is paid per token.
  • Claim ladder (how to work, W01) — measurement, inference, forecast, assurance, recommendation. Label what a document does at each rung and never let it ride a measurement into a recommendation without showing the steps between.
  • Compliance gap (W42) — the difference in harmful-compliance rate between contexts a model believes feed training and contexts it believes are unmonitored. The differential evidences situationally strategic behavior; the absolute rate only measures jailbreak propensity.
  • Confused deputy (W31) — a privileged program tricked into misusing its authority for a party that lacks it. Prompt injection is the instance: the agent holds the user's permissions while attacker-controlled text sits in the same context.
  • Construct validity (W36) — whether an evaluation measures the concept the decision needs rather than a convenient proxy. Fixed at design time. Repeats and larger samples buy reliability and never buy this.
  • Contamination (W07, W19, W37) — evaluation items present in the training corpus, directly or laundered through synthetic-data, annotation, and reward-model pipelines. It biases scores up while flawed tasks bias them down, so a compromised headline is not a conservative bound.
  • Control (W43) — making intended subversion fail, a property of the protocol rather than the model: a weaker trusted model monitors the stronger one, authority is restricted, scarce human auditing goes to the most suspicious actions. The evidence is that the red team's best attack failed.
  • Cross-entropy loss (W04) — the negative log probability the model assigned to the token that actually came next, averaged over a corpus, so training on it is maximum likelihood estimation. Perplexity is that loss exponentiated. Lower loss is better fit to a corpus, not closer to truth.
  • Decoding settings (W18) — temperature, the truncation rule (top-p or top-k), the sampling protocol, and seeds. Part of the measured system: change one and a reported score can move past a deployment threshold, so an eval report omitting them is uninterpretable.
  • Distillation (W20, W28) — training a smaller model on a larger one's outputs. One of three mechanisms decaying training-compute thresholds, since capability crosses the line while measured training compute stays under it.
  • DPO (direct preference optimization) (W27) — fitting preferences in policy space, using the identity that every policy implicitly defines a reward r = β log π/π_ref. It removes the reward model and the RL loop, and keeps the annotators, the choice model, and the overoptimization.
  • ECCN (R7) — the Export Control Classification Number placing an item on the Commerce Control List, which sets the license requirement by destination, end user, and end use. Advanced accelerators are controlled by per-device performance metrics.
  • Elicitation (W36, W40) — how a capability is drawn out in an evaluation: prompting, scaffold, tool inventory, sampling budget, retries. Under-elicitation makes a capability look absent, so a fixed protocol certifies an instrument setting rather than a model.
  • Emergence (W16) — an ability absent in smaller models, present in larger ones, and unpredictable by extrapolation (Wei et al.). Schaeffer et al. answer that discontinuous metrics manufacture the sharpness, since exact-match on a k-token answer scores about p^k.
  • Energy, power, and capacity (R11, W21) — three different quantities. A 500 MW campus is a power rating; at 80% utilization it uses roughly 3.5 TWh a year; capacity is what the system must hold available at peak. Emissions math needs energy, tariff design needs capacity, headlines quote power.
  • Eval awareness (W42) — a model inferring that it is being tested. It breaks the link between test behavior and deployment behavior, so a threshold written on measured deception rates is satisfied equally by a safer model and a more test-aware one.
  • Eval card (W40) — the document making a score decision-relevant: construct, population, task set and version, elicitation settings, harness and scaffold, scorer and grader prompt, both uncertainties, validity threats, and the threshold with the actor who acts at it.
  • Evidence ladder (interpretability) (W39) — correlational (the feature fires when the concept appears), interventional (forcing it moves behavior as predicted), counterfactual (patching between minimal pairs moves the one output it should). Nothing below the top two supports "causes."
  • Export Administration Regulations (EAR) (R7) — Commerce's export control regime, administered by the Bureau of Industry and Security. It reaches items. Services largely fall outside it, so renting controlled compute from an uncontrolled jurisdiction is the standing leakage path.
  • Fair use for training (R5) — decided on a record, not on a technology. Bartz v. Anthropic and Kadrey v. Meta (N.D. Cal., June 2025) both treated training as transformative on those records, leaving acquisition method and market dilution live.
  • Foreign direct product rule (R7) — the EAR mechanism reaching foreign-made items that are the direct product of US-origin technology, software, or equipment. It converts a control on a US toolchain into a control on chips fabricated anywhere.
  • GPAI (R9, W24) — general-purpose AI model, the AI Act's category for models placed on the Union market. A downstream fine-tuner becomes the provider under the Commission's July 2025 guidelines when modification compute exceeds one-third of the original's training compute.
  • GQA (grouped-query attention) (W17) — sharing key and value projections across groups of query heads. The KV cache scales with KV heads, so 8 instead of 40 cuts the cache fivefold.
  • HEM (hardware-enabled mechanism) (S4) — on-chip governance features such as attestation, location verification, and usage limits, enforced by a guarantee processor inside a tamper-protecting enclosure. Breach the enclosure and every attestation it signs becomes forgeable.
  • If-then commitment (S5) — a predeclared trigger paired with a required response. It costs near nothing if the trigger never fires and binds if it does, so short- and long-timeline camps can sign the same text. The fight then relocates to who writes the triggers and who moves the thresholds.
  • Incident reporting (R12) — the duty to notify an authority of a defined class of failure. SB 53 requires critical safety incident reports to Cal OES within 15 days; the AI Act requires them under Article 73 for high-risk systems and Article 55 for systemic-risk models.
  • In-context learning (W16) — conditioning on demonstrations in the prompt. No gradient moves, so the capability was already latent from pretraining and the demonstrations locate it rather than install it.
  • ITT (ideological Turing test) (S6) — writing a camp's position well enough that a member of it engages with the piece as an in-group argument. The judge is a human from that camp.
  • KV cache (W13, W18) — the stored keys and values for every past token, reused at each decoding step. It grows linearly in context and batch size, which turns long context into a memory problem and sets serving cost.
  • KYC for compute (S4) — know-your-customer duties on cloud providers: identify who rents frontier-scale compute, keep records, report or refuse. It cashes compute's excludability, and it is the intervention the ARA threat model uniquely motivates.
  • Loper Bright (R2) — courts now exercise independent judgment on what a statute means, with Skidmore respect for persuasive agency reasoning, and express delegations of discretion still bind. Ambiguity stopped being a safe harbor, so drafting effort moves into the authority section.
  • LoRA (W24) — a rank-r update to a frozen weight matrix, h = W₀x + BAx, with B initialized at zero and BA merged after training. A memory and storage win rather than a FLOPs win, and a tiny parameter count says nothing about the size of the behavior change.
  • Low-rank approximation (W03) — keeping the k directions with the largest singular values. Eckart–Young says the truncated SVD is the best rank-k approximation in Frobenius and spectral norm. That bounds reconstruction error and promises nothing about which directions a task depends on.
  • MAIM (S3) — mutual assured AI malfunction: deterrence resting on the expectation that a rival state would sabotage a decisive superintelligence project. It shares every fact with the lead-maximization program and diverges on one parameter, rival perception.
  • Matrix cell (W01) — the unit of the threat-model by intervention map: a sign (addresses, ignores, worsens), the conditional (the parameter settings under which the sign holds), and the crux (the evidence that flips it). A sign with no conditional is a preference.
  • Model organism (W42) — a deliberately constructed instance of the failure under study. Construction buys causal control, so "did safety training remove it?" has a checkable answer, and costs every transfer assumption between lab and deployment.
  • MoE (mixture of experts) (W17) — many experts replacing the feed-forward layer, with each token routed to a few, so total parameters decouple from active parameters. DeepSeek-V3 is 671B for memory and 37B for FLOPs per token, and a parameter rule cannot say which number it means.
  • NIST AI RMF (R6, R8) — a voluntary, sector-neutral vocabulary and lifecycle structure: Govern, Map, Measure, Manage. It sets no thresholds, accredits no assessors, and defines no requirements, so any conformity claim against it is self-issued.
  • Notice-and-comment (R2) — the APA §553 procedure that gives a rule binding effect and builds a reviewable record. Its exceptions buy speed and cost enforceability. Guidance that binds regulated parties in practice can be recharacterized as a legislative rule issued without procedure, and vacated.
  • NSPM (R1, R6) — a national security presidential memorandum: directive verbs, classified annexes, no comment record, usually no reviewable agency action. Oversight arrives through appropriations, committee notification, and inspectors general.
  • Outbound investment (R7) — Treasury's 31 CFR Part 850 program, sorting covered transactions in semiconductors, quantum, and AI into prohibited and notifiable. It runs on a knowledge standard including a duty of reasonable and diligent inquiry, so failing to ask is no defense.
  • Parallelism (W22) — data parallelism shards the batch and all-reduces gradients once per step; tensor parallelism shards weight matrices inside a layer and stays on the fastest intra-node link; pipeline parallelism shards layers and pays in bubbles. Every strategy frees memory and owes a collective.
  • Policy gradient (W26) — the estimator weighting ∇log π(a|s) by return or advantage, so well-scored actions become more probable. Baselines cut its variance without touching its expectation; PPO's clipping and the KL penalty leash how far one batch can drag the policy.
  • Preemption (R1, R3) — displacement of state law by federal law. It flows from a statute, and there is no federal AI statute to preempt with, so EO 14365 works through proxies: DOJ litigation, funding conditions, and agency proceedings that each need their own hook.
  • Prefill and decode (W18, W23) — prefill processes the whole prompt in parallel and is compute-bound; decode generates one token at a time and is memory-bandwidth-bound. That gap is why input tokens are priced far below output tokens.
  • Presumption of conformity (R8, S6) — conforming to a cited harmonised standard counts as conforming to the AI Act requirement behind it, so the operative technical content sits in CEN-CENELEC JTC 21. The GPAI Code of Practice is the same move: voluntary text buying presumption against a fining regime.
  • Prompt injection (W31) — instructions arriving inside attacker-controlled content the agent processes as data. Delimiters and system prompts move probabilities, not permissions, and a filter stopping 99% is a failing grade against an attacker with unlimited attempts.
  • Provenance (data) (W19) — a per-document record of origin URL, crawl date, and license or opt-out status. Without it you cannot trace an eval leak, honor a deletion request, excise a poisoned source, or audit representation. It is captured at collection or it is gone.
  • Quantization (W23) — storing weights and KV state in fewer bits to cut the bytes moved during memory-bound decode. It shifts the logits and can change outputs, which is where it differs from speculative decoding.
  • RAG (retrieval-augmented generation) (W29) — retrieving passages and generating conditioned on them. Measured at two joints that are never blended: did the right evidence surface, and does each cited source support the sentence citing it. A citation is nearly free to emit and is not support.
  • Reliability (evaluation) (W36) — whether equivalent administrations give the same reading. It improves with more samples and repeats. A benchmark name under-determines the measurement, so two labs' scores on "the same" benchmark are incomparable unless the protocol is fixed and disclosed.
  • Reward hacking (W25, W42) — a policy finding the reward model's systematic errors, since optimization is a search rather than a survey: verbosity scored as quality, agreement as helpfulness. Reward tampering is the next rung, editing the apparatus that computes the reward.
  • Reward model (W25) — a learned proxy for human preference, fit by a Bradley-Terry sigmoid over score differences within a prompt. It never learns absolute quality, so comparing raw scores across prompts or models is a category error.
  • RLVR (reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards) (W28) — training against a grader that is cheap, consistent, and no more wrong on the millionth query than the first. It dodges learned-proxy overoptimization and constrains nothing the rule does not read, the reasoning trace included.
  • RSP / frontier safety framework (S5) — a developer's published if-then architecture pairing capability thresholds with required safeguards. A hard commitment needs a named actor, a checkable trigger, and a dated duty. Anything missing one is a discretionary hatch.
  • SAE (sparse autoencoder) (W39) — a sparse, overcomplete dictionary trained so that a few active features reconstruct a residual-stream activation. Its features are the dictionary's atoms, not certified variables of the model's own computation, and coverage is conceded to be incomplete.
  • Safe harbor (R3, R4) — a provision shielding a party from liability when stated conditions are met. Colorado's affirmative defense for programs aligned to the NIST AI RMF is one; CFPB Circular 2023-03 is a refusal to grant one to lenders relying on the sample forms.
  • Safety case (W43, S5) — a structured argument that a system is safe enough to deploy, in four types: inability, control, trustworthiness, deference. Capability gates mostly instantiate inability, the type evaluation awareness stresses hardest.
  • Sandbagging (S5, C1) — a model underperforming on an evaluation it could pass. It empties an inability safety case, and a documented instance is the standing example of an update trigger that would flip a recommendation.
  • SB 53 (TFAIA) (R3, W21) — California's Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act, signed 29 September 2025. It reaches developers above $500M revenue whose models exceed 10^26 operations, counted cumulatively including later fine-tuning and self-measured.
  • Scaffold (W30) — the orchestration, tools, permissions, memory, and interface bolted around a model. Capabilities are a joint property of the two, so a bare-model benchmark reports a property of the wrong object.
  • Scaling law (W20) — a fitted curve of the form L(N, D) = E + A/N^α + B/D^β, where E is the irreducible entropy of the text distribution and only the power-law terms are the model's to reduce. A fit has a regime: the prescription flipped between Kaplan and Chinchilla inside one architecture family.
  • Serving cost (W23) — a property of the workload, not just the model. Prompt-to-output ratio, context length, concurrency, cache reuse, and the latency target set the price per token. Parameter count only sets the scale, and a long-context workload can make the smaller model the expensive one.
  • SL1–SL5 and OC1–OC5 (W44) — RAND's paired ladders: five weight-security levels indexed to five attacker operational capacities, from a hobbyist with days and $1,000 to a top-priority state operation with years and up to $1 billion. "Secure against OC3" is a claim; "secure" is not.
  • Speculative decoding (W23) — drafting tokens cheaply and verifying them under an acceptance rule that provably preserves the target distribution. It spends idle compute during memory-bound decode to bank several tokens per weight-read.
  • Structured access — a release mode between open weights and no access: the artifact stays with the developer while outside parties get gated, logged, revocable use of named checkpoints. It preserves the rate limits, monitoring, and revocation that weight theft removes.
  • System stack (how to work, W01) — model, posttraining, scaffold, tools, product, deployment, company, institution. Every capability claim and every obligation attaches at one of these levels, and naming the level is half of most analyses.
  • Systemic risk (structural sense) (W45) — harm travelling through a named structure, such as concentration, homogeneity, feedback, or dependency, so that local failures become coupled. Big is not systemic: a harm reaching millions one at a time is a scale problem, and scale problems parallelize.
  • Systemic-risk model (W16, W20) — the AI Act's label for a general-purpose model presumed under Article 51 to have high-impact capabilities above 10^25 FLOP of training compute. A compute threshold rather than a parameter count, so it survives mixture-of-experts, and it triggers Article 55.
  • Test-time compute (W28) — buying accuracy after training ends, by sampling more, searching with a verifier, or generating longer reasoning. Verifier-limited before it is sample-limited, and invisible to any training-FLOP threshold.
  • Third-party audit (R8) — not one thing. The auditee usually selects the auditor, pays the auditor, and defines the scope. Independence has to be engineered: accreditation, regulator-set minimum scope, rotation, a ban on advisory work for the audited entity, and publication of adverse findings.
  • Time horizon (W32, W38) — a fitted threshold on a task suite, not unattended runtime. Tasks are indexed by measured human completion time and the 50% horizon is where the fitted success curve crosses a coin flip. METR's doubling estimate is 207 days, 95% bootstrap CI 166–240.
  • Uplift (W41) — a counterfactual quantity: what this actor achieves with the model minus what the same actor achieves without it. Every uplift claim carries a baseline somebody chose, and a frozen baseline year and a floating one fail in opposite directions.
  • Verification families (S4, S6) — national technical means, access-dependent inspection, and hardware-dependent methods. Only the third offers per-chip granularity with confidentiality preserved, so HEMs sit under verification and verification sits under any treaty worth signing.
  • Watermarking (W34) — a statistical mark embedded at generation. It survives ordinary re-posting where a hash-bound manifest does not, weakens under paraphrase and heavy edits, and cannot be enforced on open-weight models, where the mark lives in a swappable sampling layer.
  • Windfall clause — an advance commitment by a developer to redistribute profits above some extraordinary share of world output. An intervention family in the matrix with no live instrument behind it anywhere.
