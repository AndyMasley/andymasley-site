Threat models: power, structure, and war

Read

  • Davidson, Finnveden & Hadshar, "AI-Enabled Coups" (forethought.org) — the executive summary, the three risk factors, one concrete pathway, and the mitigations in §5. Write your strongest critique before §5, then log which risk factor survives.
  • Kulveit et al., "Gradual Disempowerment" (gradual-disempowerment.ai · arXiv:2501.16946) — the mechanism, then Davidson's three objections in Thoughts on Gradual Disempowerment. State each at full strength first.
  • Kasirzadeh, "Two Types of AI Existential Risk: Decisive and Accumulative" (arXiv:2401.07836) — the distinction, the boiling-frog mechanics, and the governance section that prescribes by branch. Write your own column-by-branch list before that section, then diff.
  • Hendrycks, Schmidt & Wang, "Superintelligence Strategy" (MAIM) (arXiv:2503.05628) — reconstruct Situational Awareness's program from memory before you open it; you third-passed it in S1.

Take away

  • Threat structure determines intervention class. A decisive risk concentrates in one actor at one moment, so you gate the frontier: compute thresholds, weight security, control protocols, licensing. An accumulative risk spreads across thousands of sub-frontier deployments, so you regulate the system: concentration limits, dependency rules, epistemic infrastructure, resilience.
  • Same severity, near-disjoint intervention sets. Ask what shape a threat is before arguing about how bad it is, and ask of any proposal which shape it reaches and whether its authors accept that the other shape is someone else's job.
  • "Solve alignment and these risks dissolve." Family B runs on solved alignment: a coup needs systems perfectly aligned to one principal, and family C needs no misaligned agent anywhere. Alignment progress can move these models' probability up, so their intervention columns barely intersect the alignment stack.
  • MAIM and SA share every fact and diverge to opposite national programs on one assumption: does a rival state treat your superintelligence project as an existential threat worth sabotaging? True → deterrence and nonproliferation. False → lead maximization is the safety margin.
  • The labor evidence the intelligence-curse cousin leans on is real and small: roughly 0.2% YoY contraction in the most-exposed occupations against ~0.1% growth in the least, as of April 2026, confounded by post-ZIRP hiring contraction, offshoring, and cohort size. Spend it with that label attached.

Answers. (1) Secret loyalties, W42: Sleeper Agents shows a planted loyalty surviving supervised fine-tuning, RL, and adversarial training. Exclusive access, W44: it persists while weight security holds and the open-closed gap stays wide, which your S1 diffusion parameter puts near four months as of 2026. Singular loyalties, W43: logging, separation of duties, and external observers pointed at a misbehaving principal rather than a misbehaving model. (2) Mechanism: societal systems serve human interests because they need human labor, minds, and consent; as AI substitutes for each, those loops weaken independently and reinforce one another's drift, with no misaligned agent required. Objections: capital and taxation (humans keep asset ownership and can tax AI labor); incentive and legibility (elites have every incentive to prevent their own disempowerment, and truthful advisors to see it coming); wrong endpoint (the mechanism argues better for extreme power concentration among some humans than for universal disempowerment). The first moves GD into distribution policy: tax, transfer, antitrust. The third keeps the catastrophe and moves the row into family B's coups-and-lock-in pathway, which is why the objection comes from the coups author. (3) The bills serve different threat structures, not different quantities of one risk: the compute-threshold bill is + on decisive rows and ~0 on accumulative ones, the systemic bill the mirror image. Ranking them head-to-head smuggles in an unstated credence over threat structures. Answer with your weight on decisive versus accumulative catastrophe, and a portfolio split. (4) Row: AI-enabled great-power war, equally the China debate. Column: natsec-led national superintelligence project. SA: + (the lead is the safety margin). MAIM: − (the project is the preemption trigger). Differing assumption: rival perception. Delaney supplies the arithmetic (rival expects disempowerment ~70%, acts ~60%, US backs down ~60%, net ~25% that the dynamic delays anything six months or more), so documented sabotage doctrine raises the middle term and a credible commitment to escalate collapses the third.
