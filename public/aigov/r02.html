R2: Administrative law and regulatory design

Read

  • Administrative Procedure Act, 5 U.S.C. 551 through 559 and 701 through 706 (text) — definitions, rulemaking, adjudication, hearings, sanctions, reviewability, scope of review, remedies. Work §553 and §706 with a pen: mark which steps build a record a court can review.
  • Circular A-4: Regulatory Analysis (OMB, Sept 17, 2003; PDF) — baseline, alternatives, benefits, costs, distribution, discounting, uncertainty, presentation. Take the section order; your RIA outline follows it.
  • OMB M-25-15 (Feb 12, 2025; PDF) — revoked the Nov 9, 2023 revision of A-4 under EO 14192 and reinstated the 2003 circular. Note what changed: discount rates, distributional weights.
  • Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo (opinion) — majority and dissent on statutory interpretation, agency expertise, ambiguity, reliance, and the judicial role. Track what the majority still says a court owes an agency's reasoning.

Do

Draft a narrow rule for one documented AI risk. Build the statutory authority, evidentiary record, alternatives, cost analysis, and judicial review sections. Map the delegation you stand on, the procedure you owe, the reasoned explanation, the enforcement mechanism, the remedies, and the standard of review.

Produce three documents: a notice of proposed rulemaking, a regulatory impact analysis outline in A-4 order, and a litigation risk memorandum naming the likely plaintiff, the forum, and the strongest count against you.

Done when the rule stays within delegated authority, answers serious alternatives, preserves a reviewable record, and states what evidence would force revision.

Skip if you can already write an authority section that survives a Loper Bright reading.

Take away

  • Notice-and-comment is the price of binding effect. The §553 exceptions (interpretive rules, general statements of policy, agency organization and procedure, good cause) buy speed and cost enforceability.
  • A "guidance" document that in practice binds regulated parties can be recharacterized as a legislative rule issued without procedure, and vacated.
  • Arbitrary-and-capricious review under §706 tests the record you built, not the answer you reached. Agencies lose for ignoring an obvious alternative, passing over contrary evidence, or supplying a post hoc rationale their lawyers invented.
  • Loper Bright did not strip agencies of power, it moved the question. Courts now exercise independent judgment on what a statute means, with Skidmore respect for persuasive agency reasoning, and express delegations of discretion still bind.
  • The drafting consequence: ambiguity is no longer a safe harbor, so effort moves into the authority section.
  • Which version of Circular A-4 governs is itself a political variable. The 2023 revision lowered discount rates and added distributional weighting; M-25-15 revoked it under EO 14192 and restored the 2003 text. Show the calculation both ways.
  • For AI specifically there are few binding hooks: an existing sectoral statute (FTC Act §5, ECOA, the FD&C Act), a reporting authority like DPA §4.2, or nothing. Name the hook first, then design the rule to fit it.
Check yourself
  1. An agency issues an "AI transparency framework" as guidance to move fast. What has it given up, and how might a court treat the document anyway?
  2. Under §706, what must a challenger show, and what is the agency's best defense?
  3. After Loper Bright, where in an NPRM does the drafting effort concentrate?
  4. Which version of Circular A-4 governs your RIA today, what changed, and under what authority?

Answers. (1) Guidance binds nobody and cannot be enforced against a regulated party, and it builds no record; if it functions as a binding norm, a court can treat it as a legislative rule issued without notice-and-comment and vacate it. (2) That the action was arbitrary and capricious: an important aspect ignored, contrary record evidence unaddressed, an explanation running against the evidence, or no reasoned explanation at all. The defense is the contemporaneous record. (3) The statutory authority section; courts read the statute themselves now, so the rule must show the text authorizes it or point to an express delegation of discretion. (4) The Sept 17, 2003 circular, reinstated by OMB M-25-15 under EO 14192; the revoked Nov 9, 2023 revision had lowered the discount rate and added distributional weighting.
