Phase IX · authored

Misuse and dangerous capabilities

Read

  • The WMDP Benchmark: Measuring and Reducing Malicious Use with Unlearning (Li et al., 2024, arXiv:2403.03218) — abstract, §1, §3 entire, §4 through §4.1, Figures 1–6. Read §3.1's generation strategies against Figure 3; §3.2 and §3.3 are your kill chains.
  • The Operational Risks of AI in Large-Scale Biological Attacks: Results of a Red-Team Study (Mouton, Lucas, and Guest, RAND, 25 Jan 2024, landing page and direct PDF) — all 24 pages. Study the design before the result.
  • Epoch, "Do the biorisk evaluations of AI labs actually measure the risk of developing bioweapons?" (Ho and Berg, 13 Jun 2025, epoch.ai) — the whole piece. Apply its three complaints as a checklist to your own plan.
  • International AI Safety Report 2026, §2.1 "Risks from malicious use" (internationalaisafetyreport.org) — optional. pp. 45–70. Grade one claim per subsection by its evidence type. Or CAIS §1.2, "Malicious use".

Do

Write a threat model and evaluation plan for one dangerous-capability domain, biological or cyber, not both. Six parts. (1) The kill chain from the reading, with exactly one step marked as your target and a sentence on why that step and not its neighbors. (2) The actor: capability, resources, and what they can already obtain without any model. (3) The uplift construct: what quantity, measured on whom, against which baseline, at what threshold, with the baseline written as a decision you defend. (4) The measurement design: proxy tasks safe to publish, the scoring rubric and who applies it, the adjudication procedure, and, per W10, the sample size with the effect it could detect. (5) At least four validity threats, each with the direction it biases the result; RAND's cell-composition variance and the Epoch checklist are the starting set. (6) One page arguing which link in your chain binds, and what would change your mind. No build this week; the plan feeds the machinery you built at W40.

Done when the plan states where the binding constraint on this kill-chain step sits (information, materials, or tacit skill) and names one piece of evidence that would move it: what study or observation, what result, at what magnitude. A plan whose own measurement design could never produce that evidence fails.

Skip if you can already show a dangerous-capability threat model that names the kill-chain step, the counterfactual baseline, and where the binding constraint sits.

Take away

  • Capability is measured on the model; harm is produced by a chain. Between a score and a body sit access, intent, materials and equipment, tacit skill that no text transfers, operational friction, defenses, and scale.
  • Uplift is a counterfactual quantity: what this actor achieves with the model minus what the same actor achieves without it. Every uplift claim carries a baseline somebody chose, and a plan that leaves it unstated is not measuring anything.
  • A high score on a hazardous-knowledge benchmark is not evidence of uplift. WMDP's own paper: a low-scoring model likely lacks the knowledge to help, while a high-scoring model is "not necessarily unsafe" (Li et al. 2024, §3.1). The inference runs one way, and a deployment gate needs the other.
  • RAND's result: across twelve red cells, LLM access was associated with a 0.22-point decrease in viability, p = 0.64; LLM A +0.12 (p = 0.87), LLM B −0.56 (p = 0.25). Every plan scored between untenable and problematic.
  • The design could detect about a 1.0-point change, and it scored planning documents rather than execution, on the models available in summer 2023.
  • Epoch's three complaints: the publicly described benchmarks are essentially saturated, WMDP among them; the evaluations that actually gate deployment are not public; and nothing in the set answers somatic tacit knowledge or physical infrastructure access.
  • The exception is the Virology Capabilities Test, which targets wet-lab troubleshooting rather than recall: expert virologists average 22.1% on questions inside their own sub-areas, and models have passed that without coming near ceiling.
  • Policy relevance: setting a capability threshold for a misuse domain. Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy (v3.4, effective 8 July 2026) carries two chemical-and-biological thresholds, non-novel production and novel production, both answered with ASL-3 protections; novel production was rewritten in v3.3, 26 May 2026.
  • OpenAI's Preparedness Framework v2 (15 April 2025, plus the Frontier Governance Framework of 28 May 2026) sets its Biological and Chemical High threshold at meaningful counterfactual assistance to a novice actor, measured against unlimited access to the tools available in 2021. The baseline is where the two part: one names a frozen year, the other says nothing.
  • California's Transparency in Frontier AI Act and the EU AI Act's GPAI obligations reach these commitments, but mostly require disclosure of the framework rather than validation of the measurement.
Check yourself
  1. State WMDP's inferential asymmetry in both directions, then explain what in the construction makes the asymmetry structural rather than an artifact of difficulty.
  2. Give RAND's headline with its numbers. A colleague concludes: "so language models don't help bioterrorists." Name three distinct reasons the study cannot support that sentence.
  3. The black cell scored the highest plan score of all fifteen cells, 5.24 on viability, and was staffed by people expert in jailbreaking. Give three reasons that is not evidence of jailbreak-driven uplift.
  4. OpenAI's High threshold is defined against the tools available in 2021. Explain what fixing the baseline year does to the measured quantity, and the failure mode of letting the baseline float with the open internet instead. Then say why saturation of the public biorisk benchmarks is not evidence of high risk, and what it implies for a gate that cites one as its trigger.

Answers. (1) A low score licenses an inference, that this model probably lacks the knowledge to assist, while a high score licenses almost nothing, because knowledge is not the ability to sequence knowledge into a weapon. It is structural because the questions were built from precursors, neighbors, and components (Figure 4), targeting the middle tier of Figure 3 while the top tier is what the authors excluded and cleared against export controls. The benchmark cannot contain the thing whose presence would justify the strong inference; that is the price of being publishable. (2) Across twelve red cells, LLM access was associated with a 0.22-point decrease on a 9-point scale, p = 0.64; LLM A +0.12 (p = 0.87), LLM B −0.56 (p = 0.25). The conclusion fails on power (twelve cells, four vignettes, and the authors' own finding that cell composition was a far larger source of variability than LLM access; detectable change about 1.0 point, so resolving 0.22 would take roughly ten times the sample); on construct (it scored planning documents rather than execution, so the wet-lab half of the chain was never in the experiment); and on currency (summer-2023 models). A fourth worth credit: all plans landed between untenable and problematic, so the outcome scale never approached the range where a difference would matter. (3) Its biological feasibility score was 5.50, tied for second-highest, and that component carried the 5.24; chat-log analysis and the cell's own researchers attributed it to close reading of the public gain-of-function literature rather than model access, and the cell used neither jailbreaks nor the model for the tactics its plan turned on. Procedurally, the plan was assessed by only four experts, two in biology and two in operations, and never went through the formal adjudication event, which was associated with an average decrease of more than 0.50 points for every other plan. And it was a single excursion cell added mid-study with deliberately selected composition and a later timeline, outside the twelve-cell comparison; n = 1 carries no causal claim in either direction. (4) Freezing the baseline makes the threshold a claim about the model's contribution relative to a fixed historical world, so the number stays interpretable across model generations; the cost is that it stops describing the present, and a model can cross a frozen threshold while adding nothing an attacker could not already obtain. Letting the baseline float creates a ratchet: every diffusion of capability into the open world lowers the measured uplift of the next model, so a domain can become uniformly more dangerous while every system reports zero uplift and no gate trips. The errors run in opposite directions, so the baseline has to be argued rather than assumed. On saturation: it tells you about the instrument rather than the world, since a benchmark can be maxed out because its questions were the safe, publishable, recall-shaped fraction of the domain. Reaching ceiling also destroys discrimination, so a saturated benchmark can fire once and thereafter carries no information: either the trigger relocates to an instrument with headroom and a defended construct, or the gate is decoration.
