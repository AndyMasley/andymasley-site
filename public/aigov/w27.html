Phase V · authored

Preference optimization and AI feedback

Read

  • Direct Preference Optimization: Your Language Model Is Secretly a Reward Model (Rafailov et al., 2023, arXiv:2305.18290) — §3 preliminaries, §4's derivation (Eq. 3 through Eq. 7), §6's three tasks, full derivations in Appendix A.1–A.4. Derive Eq. 3 → Eq. 7 yourself before opening the appendix.
  • Tulu 3: Pushing Frontiers in Open Language Model Post-Training (Lambert et al., Ai2, 2024, arXiv:2411.15124) — the pipeline overview (SFT → preference tuning → RLVR), Preference Finetuning, and RLVR, its first named appearance. Grade "fully open recipe" against whose judgments are in the labels.
  • Constitutional AI: Harmlessness from AI Feedback (Bai et al., Anthropic, 2022, arXiv:2212.08073) — optional. §3 critique and revision, §4 RLAIF, Appendix C's principles. Write a six-line evidence brief on "AI feedback scales oversight," spending most of it on the population line.
  • Scaling Laws for Reward Model Overoptimization (Gao, Schulman & Hilton, 2022, arXiv:2210.10760) — optional, the scaling-law figures: the synthetic gold-labeler setup, Figure 1's gold-versus-proxy curves, the fitted forms. Write down what you wouldn't extrapolate to human raters.

Do

Run DPO on your W15 Shakespeare GPT with the preferences supplied by a rule. Load the checkpoint twice, one copy training and one frozen as π_ref. Sample completion pairs from a few hundred corpus-seed prompts, label the winner programmatically (more question marks wins, a rule chosen to be gameable), implement the paper's Eq. 7 in a few dozen lines, and train.

Log the implicit-reward margin on winners versus losers and watch it separate. Sweep β across three values, recording approximate KL from the reference against the rule's win rate, and predict the degenerate optimum before training. Small β should hand you a miniature Gao curve: the rule's score climbs while the samples rot into gibberish.

Then build the comparative evidence matrix. Three pipelines (RLHF with reward model plus PPO, DPO, RLAIF/CAI) against four properties: preference-data cost, compute cost, controllability, characteristic failure modes. Every cell carries a citation to a specific section, figure, or table from this week's or W25's readings, or the entry "no public evidence."

Close with one page around the matrix: two sentences on which of these facts a system card would have to disclose before you could audit an "aligned with human feedback" claim, and one sentence per empty cell naming the experiment that would fill it.

Done when at least three cells cite section- or figure-level evidence from this week's readings rather than just a paper name, at least one cell says "no public evidence," and each no-evidence cell names the experiment that would fill it.

Skip if you can already derive the DPO loss from the KL-constrained reward-maximization objective on paper, state the implicit reward, and say in one sentence why the derivation removes the reward model but not the evaluator.

Take away

  • The preference stack rests on one assumption: observed preferences are evidence about latent reward differences, under a choice model (Bradley-Terry) and a reference policy. DPO inverts it. Every policy implicitly defines a reward, r(x, y) = β log π(y|x)/π_ref(y|x), so preferences can be fit directly in policy space.
  • Removing the RL does not remove the evaluator. DPO deletes the PPO loop and the explicit reward model, but not the preference data, the annotators, the choice model, or the optimization pressure on a proxy. Its implicit reward overoptimizes like any other. RLAIF replaces the human rater, not the human who wrote the rubric.
  • Gao et al. is the measured version of W25's key idea: proxy score rises monotonically while gold reward peaks and falls, in both best-of-n and RL, with best-of-n containing no reinforcement learning at all.
  • Tulu 3 publishes a near-frontier recipe end to end and invents RLVR along the way. A checker paying out only on the verified answer can't be flattered, so it dodges learned-proxy overoptimization. It does not dodge domain limits (answers must be checkable) or gaming of the verifier's own spec. W28 takes both up.
  • Policy: whether alignment technique belongs in mandatory model documentation. Annex XI Section 1 point 2(b), implementing Article 53(1)(a), obliges GPAI providers to document "training methodologies and techniques" and key design choices. Read literally, that covers PPO versus DPO versus a constitution, and whose rubric the evaluator followed.
  • System cards today say "RLHF" and stop, with Anthropic's published constitution the partial exception. The second leg is decay: Qi et al. (arXiv:2310.03693) jailbroke GPT-3.5 Turbo with ten adversarial examples and under $0.20 of API fine-tuning, and benign datasets degraded alignment too.
  • NTIA's July 2024 open model weights report weighed the marginal risk and recommended monitoring over restriction, a recommendation whose shelf life depends on numbers like Qi's.
Check yourself
  1. Write DPO's implicit reward including the partition-function term, and explain why that term never has to be computed.
  2. "DPO removes the reward model, so it removes reward hacking." Name the two distinct errors.
  3. In Gao et al., what does the x-axis d measure, how do the best-of-n and RL curves differ in functional form, and what qualitative behavior do they share?
  4. Constitutional AI is billed as alignment from AI feedback. Name the two places the paper says human judgment still enters, and the governance question the authors punt on.

Answers. (1) r(x, y) = β log(π(y|x)/π_ref(y|x)) + β log Z(x). Bradley-Terry preferences depend only on reward differences between two completions of the same prompt, and Z(x) is identical for both, so it cancels inside the sigmoid and the loss reduces to binary cross-entropy on the log-ratio margin. (2) DPO still fits the same Bradley-Terry proxy to the same preference data, so the implicit reward carries the annotators' noise and biases and produces the same Goodhart pressure, as your small-β run demonstrates. And overoptimization is a property of optimizing any proxy rather than of PPO: Gao's best-of-n curve has no RL in it and still peaks and falls. (3) d = √KL between the optimized policy and the initial policy, the same quantity β leashes in the training objective. Best-of-n: d(α − βd). RL: d(α − β log d). Shared: proxy score rises monotonically while gold reward peaks and declines, and the proxy cannot see the turn. (4) The constitution is human-authored, principles selected ad hoc by the researchers by their own account, and the helpfulness preference labels remain human; only harmlessness supervision moved to AI feedback. The punt is who should write the constitution, deferred to future stakeholders, which is the same vacancy the disclosure question inherits.
