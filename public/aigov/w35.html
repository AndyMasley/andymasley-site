Phase VII · authored

World models, robotics, and action

Read

  • Mastering Diverse Domains through World Models (Hafner et al., arXiv:2301.04104) — DreamerV3: the training loop, then the results table. Rebuild the loop from memory: experience → latent dynamics → imagined rollouts → policy → environment.
  • RT-2: Vision-Language-Action Models Transfer Web Knowledge to Robotic Control (Brohan et al., 2023, arXiv:2307.15818) — action tokenization, the co-fine-tuning recipe, the ~6,000-trial evaluation. Write the evidence brief.
  • Video generation models as world simulators (OpenAI, technical report) — optional. Diffusion transformer over spacetime patches, and the emergent 3D consistency the report calls "purely phenomena of scale." Write the evidence brief; its evaluation is qualitative.
  • A Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence (LeCun, v0.9.2, June 2022, openreview.net/forum?id=BZ5a1r-kVsf) — optional, the world-model argument only: predict in representation space, push uncertainty into latent variables. Grade it as an architecture bet by an interested party.

Do

No code this week. Write a two-page policy brief on world-model and embodied deployment, addressed to a principal deciding what evidence to require before an embodied system operates around people. Three parts: what the evidence base shows (DreamerV3 and RT-2, scoped to their evaluations); demo versus deployment (lab success rates reported by the building lab versus reliability under uncontrolled conditions); where the gaps are.

Sources: IASR 2026 §1.2, subsection "Interacting with the physical world remains challenging" (pp. 29–30, on VLA systems, report PDF), and AI Index 2026 Ch. 2 for deployment-side robotics numbers.

Done when the brief separates three claim types (capability demonstrated, capability marketed, capability required for the policy conclusion) with at least one concrete, correctly sourced example of each. Blurred columns fail, and so does an empty "required" column.

Skip if you can already take an embodied-AI announcement you know and write that three-way claim split cold, with an example of each.

Take away

  • Prediction and understanding-for-action sit different exams. A video model is graded on whether typical samples look right to a viewer. A control model is graded on whether acting on its predictions works: competence under intervention, transfer under shift.
  • A system can ace the first exam and be unable to sit the second. Generating plausible futures never requires knowing which variable moves which.
  • Realistic output is not causal simulation. Fidelity is measured by human eyes on sampled outputs; causal simulation is measured by counterfactuals. No amount of the first measurement accumulates into the second.
  • DreamerV3's world model is audited by action: a policy trained inside a wrong model acts badly in the real environment, and the bad reward shows up in the results table. Sora's outputs are audited by viewing. That difference decides which literature bears on a deployment decision.
  • RT-2 shows the VLA recipe generalizes in the lab, in the building lab's own evaluation setting. Lab success rates are a measurement rung; the deployment conclusion sits several rungs up, and nothing here supplies the "capability required" column.
  • Policy: once a model moves mass near bodies, the instrument family changes from content-era tools (disclosure, moderation, provenance) to physical-safety law: product liability with its strict-liability tradition, OSHA's workplace jurisdiction, sectoral regulators with premarket and recall powers.
  • The working model is NHTSA's Standing General Order 2021-01, requiring manufacturers and operators to report crashes involving automated-driving and Level 2 systems (issued June 2021, third amendment effective June 2025). It keys mandatory evidence to the harm channel, not the content channel.
Check yourself
  1. In DreamerV3's training loop, what is the world model for, and what mechanism catches it when its predictions are wrong?
  2. DreamerV3 and Sora both get called world models. Name the difference in how each one's predictions are checked, and why that decides which literature bears on deployment.
  3. How do actions enter a vision-language model in RT-2, and what does the ~6,000-trial evaluation establish and not establish?
  4. Classify by type: (a) "our VLA generalized to novel objects across thousands of lab trials"; (b) "our robot understands the physical world"; (c) "the robot will not injure a worker when conditions shift from training." Which type does physical-safety law need?

Answers. (1) It learns latent dynamics from experience so the actor-critic can train on imagined rollouts instead of expensive environment steps. The audit is the closed loop: a policy trained in a wrong model acts badly outside it, and the poor reward is measured by the training run. (2) DreamerV3 is checked by control, Sora by viewing sampled video. The first tests whether the model's world responds to actions the way reality does; deployment is an intervention setting, so only that evidence transfers. (3) Actions are written as text tokens and mixed into the training set, so one model is co-fine-tuned on robot trajectories and internet-scale vision-language data. The trials establish improved generalization to novel objects and emergent semantic reasoning in the building lab's own setting, not reliability under uncontrolled conditions. (4) (a) demonstrated, (b) marketed, (c) required. Physical-safety law needs (c), and nothing in this week's reading supplies it.
