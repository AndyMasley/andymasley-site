Phase IX · authored

Societal and systemic risks

Read

  • AI Safety, Ethics, and Society, Chs. 5 and 7 (Center for AI Safety, aisafetybook.com) — Ch. 5 Complex Systems (§5.2, §5.3) and Ch. 7 Collective Action Problems (§§7.2–7.5). Rebuild Ch. 5's composition argument from memory before you check.
  • Multi-Agent Risks from Advanced AI (Hammond et al., 2025, arXiv:2502.14143) — §2.3 Collusion (pp. 17–20) and §3.2 Network Effects (pp. 23–26), especially "Homogeneity and Correlated Failures." Write an evidence brief on the correlated-failure claim.
  • International AI Safety Report 2026 (internationalaisafetyreport.org) — optional. §2.3.1 Labour market impacts and §2.3.2 Risks to human autonomy. Grade the retreat: 2025's environment, privacy, copyright, and market-concentration sections were dropped.

Do

Write the systemic risk register: three entries, no more, one concentration, one correlated-failure, one epistemic. Each entry carries three fields. Transmission mechanism: the route from local failure to system-level harm, drawn concretely enough that a skeptic could design the observation showing the route isn't carrying anything. Evidence grade: where the best current evidence sits on the claim ladder, citing the single strongest source; this week hands you field evidence for algorithmic collusion, a lab existence proof for steganography, structural inference for monoculture, and mostly assertion for epistemic erosion. Intervention family: the matrix column that reaches the mechanism, written in matrix language (sign, conditional, crux) or "none live," which is a finding rather than a blank. The register feeds S3. No build this week.

Done when each entry names a transmission mechanism specific enough to be falsified: for every mechanism line you can state the observation that would show the mechanism is not operating, and a reader could run that check without asking what you meant.

Skip if you can already show a three-entry systemic risk register whose mechanism lines a skeptic could falsify.

For the dossier, take the newest frontier model on the roster page and write its safety, control, and security snapshot: each claimed capability threshold, the threat pathway it gates, the evaluation behind the claim, the mitigation it triggers, the weight-security posture, and the residual risk with a named owner, ending with the fields the card leaves blank. The Phase IX gate, on the gates page, is the frontier-system audit, five pages plus a claim ledger over a current frontier system's public record. W45 plus the S-block is a designed stopping point.

Take away

  • "Systemic" is earned by a transmission mechanism: a named structure (concentration, homogeneity, feedback, dependency) through which local failures become system-level harm. If you can't draw the route from one failure to many, you have a large harm rather than a systemic one.
  • Big is not systemic. A harm reaching millions one at a time, each instance independent, is a scale problem; scale problems parallelize across existing regulators. Systemic means the failures are coupled: one cause, many simultaneous or compounding effects. Coupled problems need an instrument at the coupling point, and there usually isn't one.
  • The monoculture route: most deployed agents are wrappers or fine-tunes of a handful of foundation models with heavily overlapping training data, so a flaw in one base model surfaces near-simultaneously across nominally independent downstream systems. The evidence is structural inference from data-overlap measurements, not deployment incidence.
  • Collusion requires cooperation at a third party's expense, so correlated behavior from shared signals is a network effect rather than collusion. The German gasoline market is the field instance of tacit collusion; steganographic channels under equally capable oversight are a lab existence proof.
  • Component-level safety does not compose into system-level safety, and every register entry sits on a collective-action structure underneath.
  • Policy relevance: telling a principal which systemic risks have any live instrument, where the answer is mostly none. The EU AI Act is the one major statute using "systemic risk" for frontier AI, and it attaches the label to models above a training-compute presumption (W16's threshold), a model-level proxy while all three mechanisms live at the system level.
  • The rare live instrument is DORA, the EU's financial-sector resilience regulation: on 18 November 2025 the European Supervisory Authorities designated 19 critical ICT third-party providers, hyperscale cloud among them, for direct oversight with inspection and penalty powers.
  • Correlated failure across shared base models is reached by no authority anywhere, and the epistemic route has only W34's provenance instruments, a voluntary standard plus labeling mandates that regulate the layer W34 showed strips.
Check yourself
  1. A briefing calls mass AI-generated disinformation "a systemic risk." State this week's test, then write one version of the claim that passes it and one that fails.
  2. Classify by CAIF's taxonomy: (a) trading agents with similar strategies crash a market by acting on a common signal; (b) pricing agents sustain supra-competitive prices without ever communicating. Name where each belongs, and why (a) is not collusion.
  3. State the monoculture transmission mechanism precisely enough to falsify, and give the observation that would falsify it. Place the German retail-gasoline case and the LLM steganography study on the claim ladder, with one thing each cannot establish.
  4. Which register entry does the EU AI Act's systemic-risk designation reach, which does DORA reach, and which entries have no live instrument at all?

Answers. (1) The claim must name the structure the harm travels through, not its size. Fails: "synthetic content is everywhere, so the risk is systemic," which is a large pile of independent local harms. Passes: "synthetic content degrades the shared verification layer (provenance, trusted intermediaries) that downstream institutions all depend on, so one polluted layer propagates into every decision that consumes it," falsifiable by testing whether verification reliance and trust measures actually degrade with exposure. (2) (a) is not a failure mode at all in CAIF's taxonomy but the risk-factor side, §3.2's network effects in the homogeneity-and-correlated-failures pattern: correlated behavior from shared signals and similar strategies, with no cooperation between agents. Collusion requires cooperation at a third party's expense, and here nobody cooperated, everybody just moved together. (b) is the collusion failure mode in its tacit form, with the German gasoline market as its field instance. (3) Mechanism: because most deployed agents are wrappers or fine-tunes of a handful of foundation models with heavily overlapping training data, a flaw in one base model surfaces near-simultaneously across nominally independent downstream systems. Falsifier: run a shared adversarial input or exploit across downstream agents grouped by base model, and if same-base agents fail no more concordantly than different-base agents, the monoculture route isn't carrying the risk. Gasoline is field measurement supporting an inference, that ordinary adaptive pricing algorithms produced collusive outcomes without communication; it cannot establish that frontier-model agents will do the same, and shows nothing about intent. Steganography is a lab existence proof, that capable models can sustain hidden channels under equally capable oversight; it cannot establish that deployed agents do, or at what rate. Neither supports assurance. (4) The AI Act's designation attaches to individual models via a compute presumption, so it reaches none of the three structural mechanisms directly. DORA reaches the concentration entry: vendor concentration, finance sector only, at the level of designated critical providers. Correlated failure across shared base models has no instrument at all, and the epistemic route has only W34's provenance instruments, which fail at the mechanism, since signatures strip, watermarks weaken under paraphrase, and open weights escape both. One sectoral instrument, one column of instruments that don't hold, one bare column: that distribution is the register's finding.
