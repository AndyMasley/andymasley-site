Foundation gateway

Read

  • The Python tutorial (docs.python.org) — chapters 3–5, plus 6 and 8 if imports and tracebacks read as noise, and the NumPy quickstart. Repair for check 1; redo the pass task afterward without copying.
  • Virtual environments and packages (Python tutorial ch. 12) and Software Carpentry's git-novice (lessons) — repairs for checks 2 and 3, an evening each. git-novice stops short of branching; learn that leg from the pass task.
  • The claim ladder (How to work) — repair for check 8. Grade five claims from one system card against it, asking what decision each could and could not support.

Do

Attempt each pass task cold: fresh kernel, closed book, nothing copied. Pass and move on. Fail and take the matching repair route above, then repeat the task, still cold. Nothing in Read is required unless its check fails.

  1. Python and notebooks. In a fresh notebook, load a small CSV, clean one column, compute a grouped statistic, draw a labeled chart, and rerun the whole notebook from a clean kernel without errors.
  2. Files, environments, and reproducibility. Create an isolated environment, install pinned dependencies, save a requirements file, and reproduce your check-1 result in a new directory from that file alone.
  3. Version control. Create a repository, commit a working analysis, branch, make a change, inspect the diff, merge it, and recover an earlier version without deleting current work.
  4. Tensor shapes and matrix multiplication. Predict, then verify, the output shapes for a batch of token embeddings pushed through three projection matrices and an attention score product.
  5. Linear transformations, rank, and projections. Explain a matrix as a transformation, compute the rank of a small example, and show concretely what a projection preserves and what it discards.
  6. Probability and Bayes. Solve a base-rate problem, compute an expectation and a variance, and explain why a posterior depends on both the evidence and the prior.
  7. Derivatives and the chain rule. Differentiate a two-layer scalar computation by hand, label each local derivative, and verify the result numerically.
  8. Evidence discipline. Take one published AI claim and separate the measurement, the inference, the forecast, the assurance, the recommendation, and the missing evidence.

Done when all eight are demonstrated, passed cold or repaired and then passed cold, and recorded: one line in progress.yaml, or tell the tutor and it writes the line.

Take away

  • The gateway routes; it does not grade. A cold pass and a repair are equally good outcomes. The only bad state is an undemonstrated "I'm sure I know this."
  • Recognition is not demonstration. "I took a course on this" and "the video made sense" are the kinds of evidence this gateway exists to replace. Cold conditions are the conditions you will need these under in W13, W18, and every memo after.
  • Checks 4–7 have no external repair route because W2–W5 are the repair material. A fail means taking those positions at full speed instead of fast-passing them: W2 and W3 for checks 4 and 5, W4 for check 6, W5 for check 7. Redo check 7 afterward with a different nonlinearity, to prove it wasn't memorized.
  • Checks 1–3 re-check formally in W6, which reruns check 2 as its opening diagnostic. Repair earlier only when a gap blocks the week you are in.
  • Check 8's move, asking what decision a claim could and could not support, is the habit every artifact downstream is graded on. W1 runs it again on documents you have already read.
Check yourself
  1. You pass six checks cold but fail 1 (Python) and 6 (probability). What happens next, in what order, and what will count as evidence that each has finally passed?
  2. Why do checks 4–7 route into W2–W5 rather than to an external course?
  3. Where does check 8's repair route point, and what is the move once you're there?

Answers. (1) Nothing gets recorded yet. Check 1's repair starts now, the Python tutorial plus the NumPy quickstart, but its formal re-check waits for W6, where the pass task runs again cold and uncopied. Check 6 resolves first: don't fast-pass W4. What counts as evidence is doing the task cold, meaning fresh kernel, closed book, no copying; course history and familiarity are recognition, the failure mode the gateway screens out. (2) Those weeks are the repair material; routing elsewhere duplicates the content and loses the pass tasks that verify it. A math fail here is a signal to take those sequence positions at full speed, not a prerequisite failure. (3) The claim ladder in How to work, applied to five claims from one system card, asking of each what decision it could and could not support. W1 then runs the same grading move on the two scenario documents you re-read there.
