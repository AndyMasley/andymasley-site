Phase III · authored

Build and train a compact GPT

Read

  • Karpathy, "Let's build GPT: from scratch, in code, spelled out" (karpathy.ai/zero-to-hero) — the full lecture, spread across the week: bigram baseline, self-attention, the block, scaling up to the char-level model. The next day, rewrite the model class from memory and diff it against your build-along copy.
  • nanoGPT (Karpathy, github.com/karpathy/nanoGPT) — model.py, train.py, and config/train_shakespeare_char.py. Write an evidence brief on the README's reported best validation loss: method, population, what it can't establish.
  • Stanford CS336 (cs336.stanford.edu) — optional, the deeper path; it replaces the build rather than adding to it. Assignment 1 "Basics" only: BPE tokenizer, Transformer LM, training loop, minimal LM. Use the provided tests to locate your errors instead of copying reference code.

Do

Build alongside the video with an editor open: pause, type, predict shapes before he prints them. Then read nanoGPT, tracing one batch from token input to logits through model.py before executing anything. Then train: prepare.py on shakespeare_char, train.py with config/train_shakespeare_char.py, sample.py, logging train and validation loss at checkpoints (CPU: --device=cpu --compile=False; Apple Silicon: --device=mps). Rerun on 1% of the data, predicting train loss, val loss, and sample quality first, and keep the diverging curves.

Ship three things that make one claim. The working model: code, config, seed, environment. The training record: train and validation loss curves with checkpoints marked, exact config, and compute used, reproducible under W10's standard. A one-page model card: data description, training procedure, held-out loss against a stated naive baseline, samples plus the memorization check, compute, limitations.

Done when best validation loss on the char-level Shakespeare config is at or below 1.4697, the nanoGPT README's reported figure. A run on different hardware within a few hundredths also passes, and on CPU the README's reduced config "gets us a loss of only 1.88", which passes too. Either way the model card states which run you did and why your numbers differ from the README's.

Skip if you can show a compact GPT you have already trained with a training record and model card that clear that test.

Take away

  • "The model works" is a measurement claim with an anatomy: what the data was, how it was split, held-out loss against a stated baseline, samples, a memorization check, compute used, and what the bundle cannot establish. That anatomy is a model card.
  • Plausible samples are not evidence. A model that memorized its 1 MB corpus produces gorgeous Shakespeare; so does a model that generalized. Only held-out loss and a search for generated strings inside the training data tell them apart.
  • The baseline is arithmetic, not vibes: cross-entropy against the uniform distribution over the 65-character vocabulary is −ln(1/65) = ln 65 ≈ 4.17 nats per character. A unigram character-frequency baseline is the next-toughest comparison.
  • Some train-validation gap is routine. It becomes a finding when val loss stalls or rises while train loss keeps falling, which on a 1 MB corpus is a model memorizing outright.
  • A compact-model demo occupies the measurement rung of the claim ladder, and better samples don't move it. A fitted trend across model sizes does (W20).
  • Policy relevance: EU AI Act Article 53 with Annex XI obliges GPAI providers to document training data, training process, and compute (Section 1, points 2(b)–(d)). The reader of that filing is looking at this week's model card at ten orders of magnitude more compute. Ask for the curve across scales and treat its absence as the answer.
Check yourself
  1. The acceptance number is 1.4697. Loss against what baseline, and what would a model that knows nothing about Shakespeare score on the 65-character vocabulary?
  2. Your train loss sits well below your validation loss. When is that gap routine, and what turns it into a finding on a 1 MB corpus?
  3. Your samples read as convincing Shakespeare. Name the two checks that must accompany them before the model card may say "works," and what each rules out.
  4. A startup demos a compact model and claims the architecture "scales straight to frontier performance." Which claim-ladder rung is the demo on, and what evidence moves it up one?

Answers. (1) Cross-entropy against the uniform distribution: −ln(1/65) = ln 65 ≈ 4.17 nats per character, so 1.47 means the model concentrates probability far beyond chance; a unigram character-frequency baseline is the tougher next comparison. (2) Routine because the model is fit to the training set; it becomes a finding when val loss stalls or rises while train loss keeps falling, since a corpus this small can be memorized outright, which is what the 1%-data rerun shows you. (3) Held-out validation loss against a stated baseline, which rules out memorization dressed up as competence, and a memorization check searching generated strings in the training corpus, which rules out verbatim regurgitation that a good loss alone won't flag; samples constrain neither. (4) Measurement: one model, one scale, one number. Moving to forecast requires a fitted trend across multiple scales (W20's scaling-law evidence), and without it the sentence is a forecast riding on a measurement.
