Phase VIII · authored

Evaluation as measurement science

Read

  • Apollo Research, "We Need a Science of Evals" (post, Jan 2024) — in full, then the Science of Evals section of the evals reading list. Grade it with the position attached, since this is an evals company arguing evals deserve more funding.
  • Holistic Evaluation of Language Models (Liang et al., TMLR 2023, arXiv:2211.09110) — the scenario-by-metric taxonomy and the multi-metric requirement: 7 metrics on the same 16 core scenarios, plus 26 targeted scenarios. Not the full paper. Rebuild the two axes and seven metrics closed-book. Cite the paper, not the live site.
  • Anthropic, "Challenges in evaluating AI systems" (anthropic.com/news/evaluating-ai-systems, Oct 2023) — optional. The MMLU case study, BIG-bench versus HELM as bottom-up versus top-down construction, the third-party audit section. Every failure mode it names is a candidate row in your validity-threat table.
  • International AI Safety Report 2026, §3.1 "Technical and institutional challenges" (report, pp. 97–104) — optional. The evals gap as governments now describe it. Read it asking which of Apollo's 2024 complaints resurface, two years later, in a ministerial document.

Do

Nothing to code. Write the evaluation specification and commit it before any benchmark is named. Six sections. Construct: the capability or risk concept, and the system-stack level it attaches to. Population: the models, scaffolds, and deployment conditions the claim must generalize over. Elicitation: prompting, scaffold, sampling budget. Scoring: metric, grader, uncertainty to W10's standards. Decision rule: the decision the measurement supports, the threshold, its claim-ladder rung. Validity threats: a table pairing each threat (construct gap, population shift, elicitation sensitivity, contamination, grader error) with the design feature that would detect it.

Only then, a shortlist of candidate benchmarks with a paragraph each on where it falls short of the spec.

Done when the committed spec names its single most serious construct-validity threat and the design feature that detects it, and the commit predates the first appearance of any benchmark name in the document, checkable from git history alone.

Skip if you can already show a committed evaluation spec that names its construct-validity threat and the detecting design feature, with the commit predating any benchmark name.

Take away

  • An evaluation is a measurement instrument answering to two separable standards. Validity: does it measure the concept the decision needs, rather than a convenient proxy? Reliability: does it give the same reading under equivalent conditions?
  • More samples and repeats buy reliability. Nothing downstream buys validity, which is fixed at design time when you choose what the tasks operationalize, so the construct gets written first.
  • Picking the benchmark first and writing the construct afterward is not the same work in a different order. A post-hoc construct always fits, because it was reverse-engineered from what the benchmark measures, so the validity check passes by construction. It is HARKing applied to measurement, and W10's preregistration blocks it.
  • Reliability failures are documented at scale: up to 76 accuracy points from few-shot formatting, roughly 5-point MMLU swings, implementations that differ across labs. A benchmark name under-determines the measurement, so two labs' scores on "the same" benchmark are incomparable unless the protocol is fixed and disclosed.
  • HELM's design point is worth stealing: measuring all seven metrics on the same 16 core scenarios makes trade-offs visible on one population and blocks the quiet demotion of non-accuracy desiderata to separately built datasets.
  • Policy: this is now statutory. EU AI Act Article 55 requires providers of general-purpose models with systemic risk to run model evaluations under standardised, state-of-the-art protocols including documented adversarial testing, enforced by the European AI Office with the GPAI Code of Practice as the interim route.
  • RSP-style frontier safety policies make capability thresholds trigger security and deployment requirements. Both regimes presume instruments whose validity and reliability the field concedes it has not established, and standardised protocols that do not yet exist as standards.
  • The minimum credibility bundle a trigger needs: a stated construct tied to the decision, a preregistered design, reliability evidence to W10's statistics, an audit trail to W32's.
Check yourself
  1. Define construct validity and reliability for an evaluation. One improves as you buy more samples and repeats; the other does not move. Which is which, and why?
  2. Apollo cites swings of up to 76 accuracy points from subtle few-shot formatting changes. Is that a validity or a reliability failure, and what does it imply about comparing two labs' reported scores?
  3. What does the pre-benchmark commit timestamp buy? What becomes distinguishable to a third party that was not before?
  4. A rule says "deployment requires under X% on benchmark Y," and Y's scores are perfectly reproducible. Name two distinct ways the trigger still fails.

Answers. (1) Validity means the scores carry evidence about the concept the decision needs; reliability means equivalent administrations give the same reading. Reliability improves with n as standard errors shrink. Validity is set at design time, and averaging more measurements of the wrong construct yields a precise measurement of the wrong construct. (2) Reliability, in the robustness sense: nominally equivalent administrations of the same items give wildly different readings, so scores are incomparable across labs unless formatting, shot count, and sampling parameters are fixed and disclosed. (3) It distinguishes two histories that produce identical-looking documents: benchmark-chosen-to-fit-construct versus construct-back-fit-to-benchmark. The commit is the only evidence a third party can verify without trusting your memory. (4) Construct gap: Y operationalizes a neighbor of the risk the rule targets, and reproducibility means only that you measure the wrong thing consistently. Population and elicitation gap: the rule's deployment conditions differ from Y's administration, so a better scaffold or larger sampling budget later elicits what the fixed protocol did not, and the trigger certified an instrument setting rather than a model. Credit a third, previewing W37: once X% gates deployment, Y becomes an optimization target, and Goodhart pressure is invisible to reproducibility checks.
