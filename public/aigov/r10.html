R10: Productivity, labor, competition, and distribution

Read

  • Productivity data and measures (BLS, program page) — methods, series, revisions, industry detail, limitations. Find the industries where an AI effect would surface first.
  • National Economic Accounts (BEA, GDP data) — the output, investment, income, price, and industry tables. Locate data-center construction and software in the investment tables.
  • Cloud services market investigation (UK CMA, case page) — theories of harm, market definition, remedies, current outcome. Read one respondent submission alongside the inquiry group's finding on the same question.
  • Three causal studies: Noy & Zhang (Science, 2023), Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond (NBER 31161), METR's developer RCT (METR, July 2025). Read each design before its result.

Do

Pick one major AI economic claim in circulation ("AI is raising US productivity growth", "AI is closing entry-level hiring") and test it against public data and at least one causal study. Build the evidence book so a colleague can rerun it: series, vintage, source table, every transformation, the code. Keep observed change, attribution, and forecast in separate labeled layers.

Then produce the distributional incidence table (which workers, firms, consumers, and regions gain and lose, and over what horizon), one forecast with a resolution date for the log, and a policy options memo.

Done when the analysis states its assumptions, tests at least two alternative explanations for the observed change, disaggregates affected groups instead of averaging them, and keeps welfare judgments separate from political feasibility.

Take away

  • The ladder runs capability, exposure, adoption, task redesign, firm effect, sector effect, aggregate effect, distribution. Each rung sheds most of the effect above it. Almost every bad AI economics claim jumps from rung one to rung seven.
  • The three experiments do not agree, and that is the lesson. Noy & Zhang (about 450 professionals, mid-level writing): time down 0.8 SD, quality up 0.4 SD, dispersion compressed. Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond (5,179 support agents): 14% more issues resolved per hour, 34% for novices, near zero for the most experienced.
  • Self-reported gains are not evidence. METR (16 experienced open-source developers, 246 tasks) measured 19% slower with AI against an expected 24% speedup, and those developers still believed they had been sped up about 20%. Ask what was randomized and what was timed before you quote a percentage.
  • Measurement lags reality. BLS output per hour and total factor productivity arrive quarterly and annually with revisions, and intangible complements (retraining, process redesign, data work) depress measured productivity before they raise it.
  • Data-center capex enters GDP immediately as investment in structures, equipment, and IP products. That is growth in the accounts, not yet productivity.
  • Macro forecasts on the same evidence differ by an order of magnitude: Acemoglu puts ten-year TFP gains under 1%, Goldman Sachs put the effect near 1.5 points of annual productivity growth and roughly 7% of global GDP. The gap is assumptions about task coverage and cost per task, not a dispute about data.
  • Policy relevance: the near-term instruments are competition instruments. The CMA's inquiry group found market power in cloud and routed remedies into the strategic market status regime under the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024, so finding and remedy sit in different proceedings.
Check yourself
  1. A firm reports a 30% productivity gain from an AI tool. Name three things you need before that is evidence of an aggregate effect.
  2. Which of the three studies compress the skill distribution, which found a negative effect, and what design difference explains the sign?
  3. Where does data-center construction appear in the national accounts, and why does that mislead a productivity argument?
  4. Why did the CMA's finding of market power not itself produce remedies?

Answers. (1) Whether the comparison was randomized or self-reported; the population and task mix, since novice and expert effects differ in sign across these studies; and the firm's share of employment plus the diffusion rate, net of reallocation. Check also that the output measure is value added rather than issues closed. (2) Noy & Zhang and Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond compress, because gains concentrate in workers whose unaided output was below what the model produces. METR is negative: expert developers on large repositories they know well, where model output needs review and rework and maintainers set the quality bar. Task familiarity and the review burden drive the sign. (3) As private fixed investment in structures, equipment, and intellectual property products, which adds to GDP in the year of construction whether or not anything downstream becomes more productive. Productivity requires output per hour to rise afterward. (4) The inquiry group recommended strategic market status investigations under the DMCC Act 2024; conduct requirements would be imposed in that separate proceeding, with its own designation process and appeal route.
