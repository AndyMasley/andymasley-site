Phase V · authored

LoRA fine-tuning

Read

  • LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models (Hu et al., 2021, arXiv:2106.09685) — §§1–3, §4.1–4.2 (h = W₀x + BAx, the α/r scaling, which matrices get adapted), §5.5, §7.1–7.3. Closed-book: write the modified forward pass with every dimension attached and both initializations, then check.
  • Hugging Face PEFT, LoRA guide (docs; conceptual guide) — the common LoraConfig fields (r, lora_alpha, target_modules, bias, modules_to_save), merge_and_unload(), the initialization options. Map every field you set to the paper section that justifies it.
  • QLoRA: Efficient Finetuning of Quantized LLMs (Dettmers et al., 2023, arXiv:2305.14314) — optional. The abstract and the three innovations (4-bit NormalFloat, double quantization, paged optimizers). Write an evidence brief on Guanaco's 99.3% of ChatGPT: who judged, on what population.

Do

Fine-tune a small open-weight model onto one narrow behavior a regex can judge. Base: Qwen3-1.7B or SmolLM3-3B (both Apache 2.0, instruct variant). Behavior: every answer ending with a one-line TL;DR:, every answer being exactly three bullets, a fixed refusal sentence for one benign topic. Two constraints: the base model must do it in under one held-out prompt in five, and the judge must be a committed script rather than your eye.

Order of operations: (1) write ~60 held-out prompts plus the scoring rule and commit them; (2) build a few hundred training examples exhibiting the behavior, recording where every example came from; (3) train with PEFT, LoraConfig at r=8, target_modules on the attention projections; (4) score held-out completions pre- and post-tune. Then retrain at r=1, predicting first whether the delta survives. Table 6 says rank 1 sufficed for {W_q, W_v} on their tasks.

Ship three pieces as one claim. The adapter: weights, config, seed, training data, code. An adapter card to W15's model-card standard: base model and exact revision, adapter config (r, α, target modules), training-data description with provenance, procedure, compute used, limitations, and the behavior delta as the headline measurement. The delta report: pre- and post-tune rates on the held-out set with counts, the commit hash of the preregistered prompt set, the scoring rule, three failure cases inspected by hand. Say plainly that the bundle establishes nothing about behavior off the measured distribution.

Done when the target behavior appears in >80% of held-out completions post-tune and <20% pre-tune under the committed mechanical rule, and the prompt set's commit predates the first training run. Both facts are checkable from your repo history.

Skip if you can already show a LoRA adapter you trained, with a prompt set committed before training and a measured behavior delta clearing that bar.

Take away

  • The unit of evidence for a fine-tune is a preregistered before/after delta: a held-out prompt set committed before training, a mechanical scoring rule, and the behavior's rate on both sides of the run. Falling loss, curated transcripts, and vibes are the small-scale version of the assurance language later phases teach you to refuse.
  • A small update is not a small change. §7.3 shows the learned update doesn't echo the base model's dominant directions; it amplifies directions the pretrained weights barely used, by a factor around 21.5 at rank 4 in their GPT-3 measurements. Parameter count measures the cost of a change, not its size.
  • B = 0 at step zero means ΔW = BA = 0, so training starts at the pretrained model and the first gradient step is a measured move from a known baseline. After training, BA merges into W₀ and the deployed model is a plain dense layer, so no inference latency.
  • LoRA cuts trainable parameters and optimizer states (~10,000× on GPT-3), GPU memory (~3×), and per-task storage. It leaves arithmetic per training token roughly untouched: forward and backward still run through the entire frozen base. A memory-and-storage win, not a FLOPs win.
  • Policy: which event turns a downstream fine-tuner into a regulated provider. The Commission's GPAI guidelines (C(2025) 5045 final, 18 July 2025, §3.2) make a downstream modifier the provider of a model, inheriting Article 53 duties.
  • The indicative criterion is modification compute exceeding one-third of the original's training compute, or one-third of 10²³ FLOPs when the original is unknown (one-third of 10²⁵ for a systemic-risk model).
  • Your run sits about six orders of magnitude below that fallback line while flipping a behavior outright, so a compute-indexed trigger will essentially never fire. The live question is whether safeguards survive modification at all, which W27 takes up.
Check yourself
  1. LoRA initializes A with random Gaussians and B at zero. Why must B start at zero, and why does the finished adapter add no inference latency?
  2. Table 6 shows rank 1 already competitive when adapting {W_q, W_v}. What does that say about the update the task needed, and what r=1 result of your own would have falsified W3's low-rank premise for your behavior?
  3. Your delta is <20% → >80%. Name the two distinct failure modes that committing the prompt set before training closes.
  4. Estimate your fine-tune's training compute with W21's 6ND rule and compare it to the Commission's fallback modification threshold. What does the ratio imply?

Answers. (1) With B = 0 the adapter contributes nothing at step zero, so training departs from a known baseline instead of a random perturbation you would have to train back out; h = W₀x + BAx = (W₀ + BA)x, so BA merges after training. (2) The update those tasks required had very low intrinsic rank, a few directions carrying the adaptation, W3's fast-decaying spectrum made empirical. Falsification: your delta collapsing at r=1 and climbing materially with r, evidence that your task's update needs more directions than the low-rank bet assumes. (3) Post-hoc prompt selection, which is transcript curation by another name, and contamination of the held-out set by the training corpus; the commit fixes the eval population before the corpus exists to leak into. (4) Roughly 6 × 1.7×10⁹ × 10⁶ ≈ 10¹⁶ FLOPs for a million training tokens through a 1.7B base, against a fallback line of a third of 10²³ ≈ 3×10²², about six and a half orders of magnitude above you, while the behavior flipped. A compute-indexed trigger classifies essentially every behavior-changing fine-tune as too small to regulate, so whatever protection it was meant to provide has to come from somewhere else.
