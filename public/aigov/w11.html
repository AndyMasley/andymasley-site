Phase II · authored

Calibration, shift, and causal claims

Read

  • On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks (Guo et al., ICML 2017, arXiv:1706.04599) — §2 Definitions (reliability diagrams, ECE), §3 Observing Miscalibration, §4 temperature scaling; skip the method-comparison tables. Draw an overconfident model's reliability diagram and write the ECE formula from memory, then check against §2.
  • WILDS: A Benchmark of in-the-Wild Distribution Shifts (Koh et al., 2021, arXiv:2012.07421) — the two shift settings, plus Camelyon17, iWildCam, and CivilComments. Not the leaderboard, not the baseline tables. Keep a ledger per dataset: the shift, the deployment population, and what an i.i.d. test number would have overstated.
  • Causal Inference: What If, Ch. 1, A Definition of Causal Effect (Hernán & Robins, free PDF at miguelhernan.org/whatifbook) — individual and average causal effects, and the closing contrast between causation and association. Write E[Y | A=1] and E[Y^{a=1}] from memory, plus one sentence on why they differ in general.
  • Holistic Evaluation of Language Models (Liang et al., TMLR 2023, arXiv:2211.09110) — optional, and read the paper rather than the live leaderboard. The multi-metric argument and the targeted evaluations. Pick one scenario and state which of this week's three failure modes its metrics would and would not detect.

Do

One notebook, one classifier, two failures made visible. Plot a small image classifier's reliability diagram and compute ECE, then fit temperature scaling on a held-out split and re-plot. Evaluate the same model on a shifted test set and plot accuracy and ECE, clean versus shifted. Predict first whether the clean-fitted temperature survives the shift. D2L 4.7 is the worked reference.

Then redesign one published evaluation so its claim survives a named distribution shift. Pick one attached to a real claim: a system-card number, a medical-AI accuracy figure, a policy-document leaderboard result. One page: the original claim and its population; the deployment population and the shift in WILDS' terms; your redesign (test-split construction, per-subgroup reporting, calibration); what the original number overstated.

Done when the redesign names the shift, names the new population, and states in one sentence what the original number would have overstated. If you can't fill in the overstatement, you picked an evaluation that was already honest; pick again.

Skip if you can already sketch a reliability diagram for an overconfident model, name WILDS' two shift settings with a dataset each, and explain in two sentences why a calibrated model can still be wrong on every case that matters.

Phase II closes here. The gate asks you to defend a calibration plot, a shifted evaluation, and one correctly labeled causal claim; it is on the gates page.

Take away

  • Calibration is a frequency promise on a distribution: of all the times the model says 80%, about 80% are right. It is a property of the score on a population, never of a single answer.
  • Temperature scaling is one scalar dividing the logits, fitted by NLL on a held-out split. It can buy calibration after the fact while leaving softmax ordering, accuracy, and ranking untouched, which tells you how little calibration certifies.
  • A calibrated model can still be wrong on every case that matters. Calibration aligns stated confidence with observed frequency where it was measured. It guarantees nothing per case, and it does not survive shift; recalibration is owed after any change of population.
  • WILDS' two settings decide what the headline number should be. Domain generalization (Camelyon17's sixth hospital) calls for performance on held-out domains; subpopulation shift calls for worst-group performance, because the average is the number that hides it.
  • E[Y | A=1] compares disjoint subsets of the population, and E[Y^{a=1}] compares the same population under two counterfactual treatments, so the first tracks who got treated rather than what treatment does. They coincide under exchangeability.
  • EU AI Act Article 15 requires high-risk systems to achieve "an appropriate level of accuracy" with the metrics declared in the instructions for use, and to perform consistently "throughout their lifecycle".
  • Operationalizing that means naming accuracy on which population, calibrated by which measure, re-verified under which shifts. Camelyon17's hospital-transfer failure is what "consistent throughout the lifecycle" has to rule out.
Check yourself
  1. Your model's reliability diagram sags below the diagonal everywhere. Name the one-parameter fix, the data you fit it on, and two things it cannot repair.
  2. A tumor classifier trained on five hospitals deploys to a sixth; a toxicity filter matches training proportions overall but fails on comments mentioning specific demographic groups. Which WILDS setting is each, and what should the headline number be?
  3. A vendor says: "our model is well calibrated, so its confidence scores can be trusted for triage." Give two distinct failures this sentence leaves open.
  4. "Hospitals using our system had 12% fewer adverse events." Which claim-ladder rung is that, and what design would move it up one?

Answers. (1) Temperature scaling: a single scalar dividing the logits, fitted by NLL on a held-out validation split, never the test set. It cannot repair which cases are wrong, since softmax ordering is unchanged and accuracy and ranking stay fixed, and it does not hold under shift; your build's perturbation showed the clean-fitted temperature failing on the shifted set. (2) The sixth hospital is domain generalization, so report performance on held-out domains. The demographic failure is subpopulation shift, so report worst-group performance, because the average hides it. (3) Per-case: a calibrated 90% is wrong one time in ten, and calibration doesn't say which time. Population: calibration was measured on some distribution and the triage population is a shift away, and calibration is among the first properties shift breaks. (Construct validity, confidence about the wrong quantity, also accepted.) (4) A measurement of an association, being ridden toward a causal recommendation. Moving up requires an intervention-grade design, a randomized rollout across hospitals or a credible quasi-experiment, plus an explicit exchangeability argument; Ch. 1's notation is what lets you state what's missing.
