Phase VII · authored

Multimodal representations

Read

  • Learning Transferable Visual Models From Natural Language Supervision (Radford et al., 2021, arXiv:2103.00020) — §2, §3.1.4 (prompt engineering and ensembling), and the limitations section. Draw the training setup from memory first, then check against Figure 1.
  • The multimodal sections of the newest system card on the roster page (roster) — the stated modality list, per-modality training-data disclosure, every multimodal evaluation table, and per-modality deployment restrictions. Build the ledger as you read: one row per modality × direction.
  • FCC Declaratory Ruling FCC 24-17 (CG Docket No. 23-362, adopted 2 Feb 2024, PDF) — ¶¶2 and 5–9, plus the Chairwoman's statement. Mark which sentences are statutory interpretation and which are enforcement forecast.
  • Flamingo: a Visual Language Model for Few-Shot Learning (Alayrac et al., 2022, arXiv:2204.14198) — optional. §§1–2, the frozen encoders bridged by trained cross-attention layers. Read for what freezing preserves and what it caps.

Do

Write a CLIP zero-shot classifier in about ten lines: pipeline("zero-shot-image-classification", model="openai/clip-vit-base-patch32") from transformers (model card), run over twenty of your own photos against label sets you write yourself. CPU is fine. Perturb one variable: score bare labels, then "a photo of a {label}", then a deliberately wrong register ("a blurry security-camera image of a {label}"), predicting the ranking shifts before each run.

Then write the multimodal dossier. Pick one frontier model from the roster page and record its multimodal snapshot: the full modality × direction ledger including the empty rows, what the card discloses about per-modality training data, evaluation coverage per cell with benchmarks named, and per-modality deployment restrictions. Close with three sentences on which modality's claims rest on the thinnest evidence and what a regulator should demand first. Keep the ledger; W35's phase-ritual dossier lifts its modality rows from this one.

Done when the dossier names one modality × direction cell the card claims as a capability but never evaluates, with the claim quoted and the evaluation sections you searched listed, so a reader holding the same card can confirm both in ten minutes.

Skip if you can already show a multimodal dossier on a current roster model that clears that test.

Take away

  • Contrastive training learns one space for two modalities: embed N images and their N captions, score every pair, pull the N matches together and push the N²−N mismatches apart. Zero-shot classification falls out for free: embed the image, embed one caption per class, pick the nearest.
  • The classifier is a sentence written in English at inference time. Capability in a new domain arrives with no task-specific training and no task-specific measurement, so capability is cheap and evidence is expensive.
  • "Multimodal" is not one property. It is a ledger of modality × direction cells, where image in is not image out and speech in is not speech out, and benchmark success in one cell establishes nothing about another. Reading a modality list as an evaluation result is the common error.
  • Prompt wording moves accuracy because the text encoder saw web captions rather than bare labels. Matching the prompt to the training text distribution is the whole mechanism behind the paper's 1.3% from the template and a further ~3.5% from 80-prompt ensembling.
  • Adapter architectures bridge two frozen models, which preserves the language model's text competence and caps what can be recovered downstream: detail the frozen vision encoder never encoded is gone, and the non-text output direction is absent by construction.
  • Modality-specific evaluation and disclosure requirements are landing one modality at a time, because risk surfaces don't transfer: voice fraud runs on impersonation and consent, image risk on provenance, text risk on scale and persuasion.
  • Policy: FCC 24-17 is the cleanest instance. No new AI statute, just the Commission reading the TCPA's 1991 phrase "artificial or prerecorded voice" to cover AI-generated and cloned voices, effective the day of release, enforceable through damages provisions, state attorneys general, and a 48-state AG memorandum of understanding already in place.
  • Voice is the most policy-live modality because the fraud is present, consent doctrine is three decades old, and no one had to build an enforcer. Your dossier's empty cells are the other half: a modality-specific evaluation requirement is only writable once someone can say which cell is empty.
Check yourself
  1. In CLIP's training batch of N pairs, what does the N×N matrix hold, which entries are the targets, and why is the loss computed in both directions?
  2. Build the zero-shot classifier for a 1,000-class problem: what gets embedded, how many text embeddings, and why does rephrasing "a photo of a {label}" move accuracy?
  3. Flamingo trains only the bridge between two frozen models. What does freezing buy, and where do you predict an adapter system's gaps relative to a natively trained one?
  4. What did FCC 24-17 change, and why did it take effect on release rather than after a rulemaking? Your dossier found a claimed-but-unevaluated modality: which rung of the claim ladder is the card's modality list riding, and what would put a measurement rung beneath it?

Answers. (1) Entry (i, j) is the similarity between image embedding i and text embedding j, scaled by a learned temperature, and the N diagonal entries are the positives. Cross-entropy runs over each row (image against N captions) and each column (caption against N images), so both encoders receive gradient and neither modality is privileged. (2) The image embeds once, the class names embed as 1,000 captions written through a template, and highest similarity wins. Rephrasing moves accuracy because the text encoder saw web captions, so "a photo of a dog" sits closer to its training distribution than "dog"; the paper's numbers are 1.3% from the template and a further 3.5% from the 80-prompt ensemble, almost 5% together. (3) Freezing buys the pretrained language model's text competence intact plus cheap training, since only bridge parameters update. The gaps sit wherever the modalities must interact more deeply than the bridge permits: detail the frozen vision encoder never encoded is unrecoverable, and generation in the second modality doesn't exist. Predicting gap locations from architecture is the audit skill here. (4) Nothing was legislated and no rule was written; a declaratory ruling states what an existing statute already means. The TCPA's restrictions on "artificial or prerecorded voice" were held to encompass AI-generated and cloned voices, so prior-express-consent and disclosure duties applied the moment the ruling issued, and enforcement (FCC forfeitures, state-AG damages suits) predated it. The modality list rides at assurance, "the model does X" with no measurement beneath it; what's missing is a named evaluation on that exact cell with task population, procedure, score, and uncertainty.
