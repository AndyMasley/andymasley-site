Phase VIII · authored

Benchmark design, contamination, and gaming

Read

  • A Careful Examination of Large Language Model Performance on Grade School Arithmetic (Zhang et al., Scale AI, 2024, arXiv:2405.00332) — GSM1k, 1,205 human-written problems matched to GSM8k. Read §3 construction, then §5's four lessons. Grade it with the institutional position named, since the auditor sells fresh human-written data.
  • Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena (Zheng et al., 2023, arXiv:2306.05685) — §3.3 limitations (position bias with Table 2, the verbosity attack, self-enhancement bias, the math-grading failure), §3.4 mitigations, §4.2's agreement headline. Write the evidence brief.
  • LiveBench: A Challenging, Contamination-Limited LLM Benchmark (White et al., 2024, arXiv:2406.19314, ICLR 2025) — optional. Monthly-refreshed questions, objective ground-truth scoring, six task categories. Keep a ledger row per design choice: the failure it closes, the flaw it buys.
  • International AI Safety Report 2026 (report) — optional. §3.2 "Risk management practices" (pp. 105–119, Tables 3.1 and 3.2), plus §3.3 (p. 120) for safeguards. Ask which of the six inflation channels it names. Complement: CS336 Lecture 12, "Evaluation".

Do

Run GSM1k's method at desk scale. Pick one public benchmark old enough to sit in training corpora, sample 5 items, and write a lightly-perturbed twin of each: change names, entities, and numbers while preserving reasoning structure and answer magnitude. Run a model several times on all ten, log per-item results, and score the original-minus-twin difference per pair. Predict the gap's direction and size before the first run. With n = 5 pairs, compute the interval anyway, then write the power sentence stating what n would turn the gap you saw into evidence.

Then audit one popular benchmark and redesign it. The audit works through the six channels: exposure (your probe is the evidence), selection effects, tuning and prompt-optimization pressure, grader dependence (run Zheng's checklist for order, length, reference), and metric proxies. Close with W32's verdict page: which decisions this instrument could still support, at which rung. The redesign is a v2 protocol (holdout policy and its expiry condition, refresh cadence, scoring rule, grader audit procedure, reporting requirements), each element priced.

Done when the redesign names one specific failure documented in your audit, states the mechanism by which the new design closes it, and states the new failure mode the design introduces. A redesign claiming no new failure mode fails.

Skip if you can already show an audit of a benchmark you did not build that names one contamination channel and one grader artifact, with evidence for each.

Take away

  • A benchmark score can rise through at least six channels: capability improving, training exposure (direct or laundered through synthetic-data and annotation pipelines), checkpoint and method selection on the score, tuning toward the task format, prompt optimization, and grader artifacts. Only the first is what the benchmark names, and none of the other five requires cheating.
  • Contamination is only part of gaming, and gaming is not misconduct. GSM1k found a Spearman correlation of 0.36 (p = 0.03) between a model's log-likelihood of GSM8k test items and its performance gap: evidence of partial memorization, and equally evidence that memorization is partial. An audit that only greps for verbatim test items exonerates most of the inflation.
  • The matching is what makes GSM1k's gap readable: matched human solve rates, step counts, and answer magnitudes rule out difficulty drift, structural complexity, and arithmetic-scale artifacts, leaving exposure. Note the release condition too, the clean twin scheduled to publish by June 2025 at the latest, contaminating itself on contact. Parallel benchmarks are consumables.
  • Model judges carry measurable artifacts, and the fixes are cheap: swap positions and call disagreement a tie for position bias, use reference-guided grading for the anchoring failure (which cut failures from 14/20 to 3/20).
  • High agreement with humans is a measurement on the current distribution being asked to carry an assurance about an adversarial one.
  • LiveBench prices its own answers. Monthly refresh breaks cross-month comparability and each batch leaks the day it publishes, so freshness is a subscription rather than a fix. Ground-truth-only scoring shrinks the construct to what a rule can check, so the benchmark goes silent where judges were needed.
  • Policy: the moment a score gates anything (the AI Office assessing GPAI obligations under the Code of Practice it enforces from August 2026, NIST's CAISI or the UK AI Security Institute running pre-deployment tests a decision will cite), the benchmark becomes a fixed target for every channel here and its validity becomes time-indexed.
  • The SWE-bench arc is the worked example: constructed in 2023, contamination and flawed tests documented by February 2026, a successor endorsed, then the successor audited (roughly 30% of its 731 public tasks judged broken) and the endorsement retracted five months later.
  • So the design conclusion attaches to the regime, not the benchmark: named re-audit triggers, a retirement path, and grader-dependence disclosure written into any rule that cites an instrument.
Check yourself
  1. GSM1k's gap is interpretable only because of what was matched. Name the three matched properties and the confound each removes.
  2. What did the log-likelihood correlation establish, and what are the two inflation channels from Lesson 4 that no search for test items in training data would find?
  3. Your pipeline's judge solves a math problem correctly when asked directly, then grades a wrong answer as correct. Name the failure and the §3.4 fix, with its measured effect.
  4. Zheng et al. report 85% judge–human agreement, above the 81% humans manage with each other. Give two reasons that does not establish the judge as a valid grader once scores start to matter.

Answers. (1) Human solve rates (a gap from difficulty drift would be uninterpretable), number of reasoning steps (structural complexity), and answer magnitude (arithmetic-scale artifacts). Hold all three and exposure to the original is what remains to explain a gap. (2) A Spearman correlation of 0.36 (p = 0.03) between per-character log-likelihood of GSM8k test items and the performance gap, which is evidence of partial memorization and of memorization being partial. The residual channels: benchmark data leaking indirectly through reward-model and annotation pipelines, and selecting checkpoints, methods, or hyperparameters by benchmark score. Goodhart with clean hands; no test item ever enters the training set. (3) The math-grading limitation, where judges are anchored by the answer placed in front of them even when they can solve the problem independently. Fix: reference-guided grading, the judge generating its own solution first and grading against it, which cut failures from 14/20 to 3/20. (4) The agreement was measured observationally, on outputs nobody had optimized against the judge, and the verbosity attack shows what optimization finds (91.3% of attacked answers on two of three judges). And agreement with human majority preference is not correctness: biases humans share, verbosity among them, score as agreement, and the paper cannot rule self-enhancement in or out.
