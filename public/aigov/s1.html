World models and methods

Read

  • Thorstad, "Against the singularity hypothesis" (philpapers.org/rec/THOATS-5) — the growth-premise attack, plus his replies to the leading philosophical defenses. Name which of your twelve parameter settings change if he is right.
  • Greenblatt, "Plans A, B, C, and D for misalignment risk" (blog.redwoodresearch.org) — the five rungs from international agreement (A) down to nothing (E), with P(plan) and P(takeover | plan) on each. For each rung, name the parameter its takeover estimate leans on hardest.
  • AI Futures Project, "Q1 2026 timelines update" (blog.aifutures.org) — the median move and its four drivers. Write your own automated-coder median before you open it, then grade yourself against W1b's scorecard.
  • Third pass: AI 2027 + Situational Awareness (ai-2027.com · situational-awareness.ai) — both documents, marked up. For each, list five claims you now evaluate differently and the week that changed you.

Do

Label the method behind every strategy document before you judge it: contingency ladder (Plans A–E); branching scenario (AI 2027, MIRI); if-then triggers (Karnofsky, RSPs); portfolio over worldviews (Core Views, d/acc); fault tree (Barrett & Baum's ASI-PATH); backward chaining (A Narrow Path); assumption-explicit decomposition (DeepMind); single-premise reductio (IABIED).

Label five documents before S2, the two re-reads included. Note where one runs two methods and hides the seam.

Write the timelines-and-parameters memo. Twelve parameters: timelines, takeoff shape, polarity, alignment-difficulty distribution, offense-defense balance per domain, diffusion rate, compute concentration, warning-shot legibility, institutional response capacity, verification feasibility, delegation depth, labor substitution.

Operationalize each as a claim you could be wrong about and enter it in the cruxes.md format: C<n>. <claim> — p=0.XX (YYYY-MM-DD) — would move me: <evidence>. Add a timelines position: your median for one named capability milestone with resolution criteria, plus the methodology choice it is most sensitive to. Name which of the eight methods the memo itself uses.

Done when every parameter carries a credence, a date, and one named observable that would move it, and the memo names its own method.

Skip if you already maintain a dated table of all twelve with credences and observables, plus a timelines position with resolution criteria and a named method.

Take away

  • A world model is twelve dials; a strategy is a method applied to a setting of them. Backward-chain from short timelines and you get lab security this quarter; from long timelines, institution-building.
  • When two serious documents share the facts and reach opposite programs, the disagreement is usually about a parameter rather than values. RAND states the field's version: policymakers argue past one another because they weight the threat models differently.
  • The whipsaw does not discredit the forecasters. The same team moved its medians later in mid-2025 and roughly 1.5 years earlier in Q1 2026. Point medians are dominated by a few methodology choices, so the deliverable is a distribution plus its sensitivity, not a year.
  • Plans A–E conditions on political will rather than threat model, a variable most worldview documents don't carry at all. The lettering is now shared field vocabulary (AI 2040 plans in the same letters), which is adoption of a method rather than independent convergence.
  • The parameters are the live policy fights. Preemption bets on institutional response capacity; export controls price compute concentration against diffusion; the open-weights fight is offense-defense plus diffusion; every RSP is a verification-feasibility wager. S4 asks for these numbers.
Check yourself
  1. Classify by method: (a) phased prohibitions derived from "no uncontrolled superintelligence for 20 years"; (b) assumptions numbered up front, each mitigation traced to one; (c) a fork at a single decision node into two endings; (d) every marginal measure rejected because one conditional makes them all insufficient.
  2. Plans A–E conditions on political will rather than threat model. What does that indexing buy, and which parameter still does the most work inside each rung's takeover estimate?
  3. State Thorstad's central attack in one sentence, name the parameter it prices directly, and name one downstream parameter that shifts if he is right.
  4. The same team moved its medians later in 2025 and earlier in Q1 2026. Name two methodology choices that dominate such medians, and say what your memo does about that class of problem.

Answers. (1) Backward chaining (A Narrow Path); assumption-explicit decomposition (DeepMind); branching scenario (AI 2027); single-premise reductio (IABIED). (2) Indexing on will turns "which plan" from a values fight into a forecast: you can hold one threat model and still plan across all five rungs. Inside each rung the alignment-difficulty distribution does the most work; the 7%-to-75% climb in takeover risk as safety effort thins is what a distribution concentrated on "hard but tractable with effort" looks like. (3) Sustained superexponential intelligence growth is asserted rather than argued, against a record of diminishing research productivity and binding bottlenecks. It prices takeoff shape. Downstream, a slower and more continuous takeoff raises warning-shot frequency and gives institutional response capacity time to matter. (4) Which horizon-trend version you extrapolate and which doubling-time estimate you adopt; the task horizon the milestone must clear at 80% reliability is the third, and lowering that bar moved one author's median as much as the trend data did. Publish sensitivity rather than a point.
