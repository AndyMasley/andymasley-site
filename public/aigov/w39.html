Phase VIII · authored

Interpretability and causal evidence

Read

  • Scaling Monosemanticity (Templeton et al., Anthropic, May 2024, paper) — what an SAE feature is, how specificity and steering test it, the safety-relevant features, the paper's own caveats. Not end to end. Grade it with the position named, since this is a lab grading its own model.
  • On the Biology of a Large Language Model (Anthropic, March 2025, paper) — attribution graphs on Claude 3.5 Haiku: the multi-step reasoning example, then Planning in Poems and Chain-of-thought Faithfulness, then Limitations in full. Write an evidence brief per case study.
  • A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits (Elhage, Nanda, Olah et al., Anthropic, Dec 2021, paper) — optional, several sittings of real mathematics. The residual stream as a communication channel, QK/OV decomposition, induction heads. Rebuild the residual-stream picture closed-book.
  • Interpretability Illusions in the Generalization of Simplified Models (Friedman et al., ICML 2024, arXiv:2312.03656) — optional. Simplified proxies (SVD, clustering) that match the model on-distribution and misrepresent it under shift. File "interpretability illusion" with its citation.

Do

Install transformer-lens, load gpt2-small, and work the Main Demo far enough to cache activations and register a hook. Then patch: pick a clean/corrupted prompt pair differing in one token with different correct completions (the name-swap indirect-object sentence is the classic), cache the clean run, and re-run the corrupted prompt while patching the clean residual stream back in at each layer × position. Plot how much of the clean logit difference each patch restores. Swap patching for zero-ablation at your strongest site, write the predicted difference down first, and explain what you get.

Then write the evidence ladder, one page. Pick one claimed feature or circuit: the mover your heatmap localized, or a published claim at full strength. State the claim precisely (which mechanism, influencing which behavior, on what inputs), list every piece of evidence you have or the paper offers, place each on a rung with one sentence of justification, and give each the specific experiment that would upgrade it one rung. Close with the verdict line: the strongest causal sentence the current evidence supports, written so a policy reader cannot round it up.

Done when the ladder places at least three distinct evidence types on named rungs, each paired with the concrete experiment that would move it up one rung. An upgrade line that says "more evidence" fails. Rungs from your own build resolve to the committed notebook and heatmap; rungs from a paper cite the section reporting the evidence.

Skip if you can already take three pieces of interpretability evidence (a max-activating example set, a steering demo, a patching result), place each on the ladder cold, and name the experiment that upgrades each one rung.

If the week hooks you, ARENA Chapter 1 is the deep route.

Take away

  • What turns an interpretability observation into a causal claim is a controlled intervention on the proposed mechanism that predictably alters the relevant behavior while excluding alternatives. Nothing weaker supports the word "causes."
  • Three rungs. Correlational: the feature fires when the concept appears (max-activating examples, probes, graph-reading). Interventional: forcing the feature changes behavior in the predicted direction (ablation, clamping, steering). Counterfactual: patching between two minimally different inputs moves the one output it should move and leaves the rest.
  • A feature that fires on deception text is not a deception detector. SAE features are atoms of a dictionary optimized for sparse reconstruction, with no guarantee they are the variables the model computes with, and coverage is admittedly incomplete. "We found the X feature" is a hypothesis generator; the intervention is the test.
  • Scaling Monosemanticity climbs to rung two at production scale: clamping a feature and watching behavior follow is an intervention performed on a deployed model, which is what makes it more than feature-hunting on toy models.
  • An unvalidated attribution graph sits on rung one, a structured correlational hypothesis, because it is computed through a replacement model that incompletely captures the original. The biology paper tests its graph predictions by suppressing or injecting the implicated features on the real model, and reports a hit rate of roughly a quarter of prompts tried.
  • Policy: whether interpretability evidence belongs in a frontier safety case, and at what weight, is live. The International AI Safety Report 2026 treats safety cases among standard risk-management practices (§3.2), and developers regularly offer interpretability as a future basis for assurance.
  • The current answer is a supporting exhibit with its coverage and validity caveats attached, given the quarter-of-prompts hit rate and the conceded replacement-model gap. That self-report is also the standard to demand: any interpretability exhibit in a safety case should state its evidence rung and its coverage, and one that will not can be rejected.
Check yourself
  1. Mechanically, what is an SAE feature, and why is "we extracted 34 million features" not the same claim as "we understand the model"?
  2. Place on the ladder and name each one's upgrade: (a) a feature's top-activating examples are all sycophantic praise; (b) clamping that feature high makes the model flatter the user across contexts; (c) patching the feature's activation from a sycophantic prompt into a matched neutral prompt flips that response to flattery while unrelated completions stay fixed.
  3. In your build, why patch activations from a matched counterfactual prompt instead of just zeroing the component?
  4. A safety case asserts: "our interpretability audit found no deception features active during evaluations, so the model was not deceiving." Give two independent reasons this cannot carry the weight, and the defensible sentence that could replace it.

Answers. (1) A direction in a sparse, overcomplete dictionary trained so that a few active features reconstruct the residual-stream activation. They are the dictionary's atoms, not certified variables of the model's own computation, and the paper concedes incomplete coverage and no rigorous test that features match the model's real intermediates. (2) (a) Correlational, since firing tracks a human-chosen probe set; upgrade by intervening on the feature with a predicted behavioral direction. (b) Interventional, since behavior moved under forcing; upgrade to counterfactual with a matched minimal pair, one predicted specific effect, and unrelated outputs demonstrably unchanged, because steering that changes everything proves influence rather than mechanism. (c) Counterfactual, the top rung for one input; the upgrade is breadth rather than rung, replicating across prompts, seeds, and model scales with the direction predicted in advance. (3) Zeroing puts the network in a state it never occupies, off-distribution in W8's sense, so the behavior change may measure the weirdness of the broken state rather than the component's role. Patching substitutes a value the model does produce on the counterfactual input, a controlled comparison where the only difference is the information under test. (4) Coverage: the audit searched the dictionary the SAE happened to learn, and absence from the dictionary is not absence from the model. Validity under shift: a proxy faithful on the evaluation distribution can misrepresent the model elsewhere, which is the interpretability-illusions result, and deployment is elsewhere. Defensible replacement: "interpretability evidence, at stated coverage, found no positive indication of deception on the evaluated distribution, offered as supporting evidence and not as an inability claim."
