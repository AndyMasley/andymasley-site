Phase I · authored

Linear algebra as transformations

Read

  • 3Blue1Brown, Essence of Linear Algebra (3blue1brown.com) — core: "Linear combinations, span, and basis vectors," "Linear transformations and matrices," "Matrix multiplication as composition," "Inverse matrices, column space and rank," "Change of basis." Draw where î and ĵ land for one 2×2 matrix, then verify in code.
  • Mathematics for Machine Learning (Deisenroth, Faisal, Ong; mml-book.com) — core, most of the week's load: Ch. 2, 2.1–2.7, with weight on 2.6 Basis and Rank; Ch. 3, 3.1–3.8. Rebuild the 3.8 projection derivation closed-book with every dimension labeled.
  • Dive into Deep Learning (d2l.ai) — core, at the keyboard: 2.1 Data Manipulation, 2.3 Linear Algebra, appendix 22.1 Geometry and Linear Algebraic Operations. Predict the output shape before every change you make to a tensor example.

Do

One fresh notebook, four sections, every figure generated by your code. Before each rerun, predict the picture or the rank from the matrix entries alone, then look.

  1. Transformations. Push a grid of 2D points through at least four matrices, including a rotation, a shear, and a rank-1 collapse, plotting before and after each. Compose two and confirm the order matters. Check every rank claim with np.linalg.matrix_rank.
  2. Basis change. One vector's coordinates written in two bases, with the round trip shown.
  3. Rank. A full-rank and a rank-deficient 3×3, with a plot of what the deficient one collapses, plus a 3D-to-2D projection.
  4. A tiny embedding space. About 20 words as hand-built feature vectors (five or so dimensions: animacy, size, whatever you choose), projected to 2D and plotted with labels.

Done when you can point to two words the section-4 plot places close together and show, from the full vectors, that a discarded dimension separates them. If no such pair exists, change the projection until one does, and write down what you dropped.

Skip if you can already explain P = [[1, 0], [0, 0]] as a transformation with a picture, state its rank, name what it keeps and what it throws away, and do the same for a rank-2 3×3 of your own construction.

Take away

  • A matrix's columns tell you where the basis vectors land, and linearity does the rest. Composition is one move after another, rank counts the dimensions that survive, and a projection flattens space onto a subspace. A neural layer is a learned choice of which directions to keep.
  • Knowing the recipe is not knowing the object. Row-times-column arithmetic tells you how to compute Ax, not what A does. An explanation that never says where space goes is bookkeeping, and W13's projections will read as symbol soup.
  • The projection you draw this week is the Q/K/V projection with learned entries. W12's embeddings are the rows of one such matrix, and W3 asks which directions of a transformation carry the action.
  • Decision: certify or challenge a technical briefing's description of embeddings, projections, or model shrinkage. Article 51's 10²⁵ FLOP presumption assumes big capabilities require big models, and distillation is the standard route around that assumption.
  • The Hamburg DPA's discussion paper Large Language Models and Personal Data (15 July 2024; English PDF) argues that storing an LLM is not processing under GDPR Article 4(2) "because no personal data is stored in LLMs".
  • The EDPB's Opinion 28/2024 (17 December 2024) takes the contrary position: a model trained on personal data cannot in all cases be treated as anonymous, and anonymity must be shown case by case against extraction and inference attacks.
  • The disagreement is technical before it is legal. The operative question in both is what the map discarded and what test would show it.
Check yourself
  1. P = [[1, 0], [0, 0]]: what does it do to the plane, what is its rank, and what does it destroy, recoverably or not?
  2. A is 4×3 and B is 3×2. What does AB map from and to, what is the largest rank AB can have, and why can composing maps never raise rank?
  3. Two analysts plot the same 20 word vectors in 2D and get visibly different pictures; neither made an error. What happened, and which claims should you trust?
  4. Attention computes XW_Q, XW_K, XW_V, the same X through three learned matrices. From this week alone: why push the same vectors through three different projections instead of using X directly?

Answers. (1) It flattens the plane onto the x-axis, (x, y) → (x, 0). Rank 1: one dimension survives. The y-coordinate is destroyed unrecoverably, since infinitely many inputs share each output. (2) AB maps R² to R⁴, read right to left with B first. Its rank is at most 2, the minimum of the dimensions and ranks involved: B's image is already at most a 2D subspace, and A can move that subspace but cannot add dimensions to it. Transformations collapse space; none invent it. (3) They projected onto different 2D subspaces, and each picture shows what its projection preserves. An orthogonal projection never increases distances, so separation visible in either plot is real, while closeness may be an artifact of a pair the map merged. Anything beyond that gets checked in the full-dimensional space. (4) A projection selects which directions of a space to expose. Three different matrices let one vector show three faces, three learned answers to "which features of this vector matter right now," and that is the mechanism W13 builds on.
