International instruments and verification

Read

  • Baker, "Nuclear Arms Control Verification and Lessons for AI Treaties" (arXiv:2304.04123) — the mechanics that transfer: declarations, on-site inspection, unique identifiers, Votkinsk-style perimeter monitoring, managed access. Run a two-column analogy audit, transfers / breaks, writing the breaks column first.
  • Sheehan, "China's AI Regulations and How They Get Made" (carnegieendowment.org) — the policy funnel and the three binding regulations (recommendation algorithms 2021, deep synthesis 2022, generative AI 2023). Write the CAC's own brief for them without using the word "race".
  • Concordia AI, "State of AI Safety in China, 2026 edition" (concordia-ai.com · aisafetychina.com) — the executive summary and the domestic-governance chapter. List five claims that would surprise your natsec-hawk persona, marking which decide "would Beijing sign a verified agreement?"
  • AI Futures Project, "AI 2040, Plan A" (ai-2040.com) — read as its authors insist, "primarily a recommendation, not a prediction": total research transparency, mutual datacenter verification, controlled compute growth, "mutually assured compute destruction" as enforcement, a pause at expert-level AI before a ~2040 resumption. Price each component.

Do

Write four paragraphs against the record, one per case: New START mechanics, the IAEA's continuity of knowledge stress-tested to failure in Iran, the BWC verification collapse, Montreal's preconditions. Each names the single most transferable design and the disanalogy that carries the weight. Go deep enough to audit anyone's analogy, Baker's and Plan A's included.

Build the ten-instrument table: the Bletchley–Seoul–Paris–New Delhi summit declarations, the EU AI Act, the GPAI Code of Practice, lab RSPs, California's SB 53, US export controls, the Hiroshima Process reporting framework v2, the UN scientific panel, the CAC generative-AI measures, New START. Classify each binding / voluntary / declaratory, with current status from the <a href="sources.html">source register</a> and the rows it lacks logged.

Then matrix v2, curated to roughly fifty cells in the matrix/matrix.md format: the seven debate cells, plus for each threat model the intervention it uniquely motivates and one it actively disfavors. Four fights arrive in draft (pause↔continue and control↔alignment from S2, China from S3, evals-sufficiency from S5); finalize those rather than rewriting them.

Write the remaining three fresh, each as a fight over its named parameter: open weights is irreversibility × who-diffuses-first, AGI-is-far is task-horizon extrapolation validity, safety-washing is who sets thresholds. Date every credence, take crux resolution dates from the dossiers' 12-month predictions, and cut any cell you couldn't defend to a named reader.

Plan A gets one paragraph in the front matter, priced component-by-component against the four records. Write the China capability-and-governance brief: two pages with named sources on capability (Ding's diffusion frame), what the regulations bind, what the state's moves signal (WAICO, founded in Shanghai in July 2026), and a bottom line on "would Beijing sign?" with a dated credence.

Done when every maintained cell carries sign, conditional, and crux; every instrument row names its enforcement mechanism or says "none"; and the brief never asserts a bind without naming the instrument and organ that enforces it, or a signal without naming its audience. A matrix where every sign is positive fails, as does a brief in which an undifferentiated "China" does things only the CAC, MIIT, or the Politburo could do. The China-scholar ideological Turing test with a human judge sits at this gate; take the dossier snapshot before you sit it.

Skip if you already hold a conditional map covering the seven debates with sign, conditional, and crux, plus a China analysis built on primary Chinese texts. Take the week as pure finalization.

Take away

  • Verification is designed around what is countable and attributable, not around what is dangerous. New START counted deployed delivery vehicles and never verified warhead stockpiles. Every AI-treaty proposal answers the same question first, and the only serious answer so far is chips and facilities.
  • "Voluntary" is not a synonym for toothless, and "binding" is not a synonym for enforced. The GPAI Code of Practice is voluntary, and signing it buys presumption of conformity with a regulation that fines. The BWC is a ratified treaty that has run fifty years without a verification protocol. Classify on the mechanism, not the label.
  • Baker's analogy breaks in two places: the regulated parties are private firms rather than a handful of state programs, and trained weights copy at zero marginal cost, so there is no conserved physical referent for "a model". Political will is the third break, and the New START expiry (February 5, 2026) priced it in public.
  • The open-weights camps inverted in 2025–26. Washington debated restricting Chinese open models while Beijing moved to restrict its own models' export, so openness became a state instrument on both sides and the marginal-risk question is now conditional on whose stack diffuses.
  • The instrument table is lever-level fluency and the matrix is the skeleton of the capstone's position paper. R9 turns this into a negotiation brief with red lines and fallbacks; R10–R12 add the economics, infrastructure, and implementation mechanics.
Check yourself
  1. Baker's case that AI verification is more nuclear-shaped than bio-shaped rests on one structural fact. Name it, then name two facts about the AI object the analogy cannot absorb.
  2. The GPAI Code of Practice and the Hiroshima reporting framework are both "voluntary". Why do they classify differently once you name the mechanism?
  3. State the two grounds of the 2001 US rejection of the BWC protocol, the two Montreal features that transfer to AI, and the Montreal precondition AI most clearly lacks.
  4. Name two obligations the CAC generative-AI measures enforce and one function the regime serves beyond enforcement, and say what makes the algorithm registry the reusable tool.

Answers. (1) Frontier AI runs on thousands of highly specialized, physically trackable, supply-chain-concentrated chips, so a countable attributable object exists, as with delivery vehicles and fissile material and unlike bioweapons' commodity equipment. It cannot absorb that the regulated parties are private firms rather than a handful of state programs, and that trained weights copy at zero marginal cost, leaving no conserved referent for "a model", so verification must target production and facilities, the Votkinsk move, never the artifact. (2) "Voluntary" is a label; the mechanism is the classification. Code of Practice adherence buys presumption of conformity with the EU AI Act's binding GPAI obligations, a voluntary on-ramp into a fining regime enforceable from August 2026. The Hiroshima framework v2 is transparency reporting with no conformity hook: reputational, which is "none" with better stationery. (3) Rejection grounds: a protocol too weak to catch cheaters would create false assurance, and inspections would expose proprietary and biodefense information, the objection that recurs verbatim when labs face audits. Montreal transfers adjustment procedures that tighten schedules without re-ratification, and trade measures against non-parties, market-access enforcement that maps onto compute supply chains better than inspections do. The missing precondition: a harm with no strategic-competition dimension and an industry holding patented substitutes. (4) Binds: filing models and algorithms in the registry, and content-security obligations, namely review, labeling, and synthetic-media marking with real takedown and licensing consequences. Beyond enforcement, the regime signals ideological control domestically and governance leadership internationally. The registry is reusable because it was built once under the 2021 algorithm regulation and every subsequent regulation bolts new duties onto the same filing infrastructure.
