The matrix

A cell is one threat model set against one intervention family. It carries a sign (serves, ignores, or worsens), the conditional naming the world-model settings under which that sign holds, and the crux, the observation that would flip it.

Nineteen threat models by thirty-six intervention families is 684 cells. The maintained set is capped at roughly 50: the seven live debates, plus for each threat model the policy it uniquely motivates and the policy it actively disfavors. Value density collapses outside the contested cells, so completing the grid is not the goal and appears in no milestone.

The axes

Rows: 19 threat models, four families, sorted by what has to go wrong. Full dossier seeds in depth_threatmodels.json.

  • A. The AI's objectives. Fast-takeoff takeover · scheming · reward-hacking to emergent misalignment · WFLL-II correlated failure · rogue replication
  • B. Humans using AI against humans. Misuse-bio · misuse-cyber · AI-enabled coups · authoritarian lock-in · great-power war
  • C. Structural drift. WFLL-I proxy lock-in · gradual disempowerment · the intelligence curse · epistemic collapse · multi-agent flash dynamics · evolutionary selection · value lock-in
  • D. Meta-failures. Safety-tax collapse · s-risks

Columns: 36 intervention families, sorted by where in the causal chain they bite. The names below are the ones the seeded cells touch; depth_proposals.json carries the full set with July-2026 status and design cruxes.

  • Inputs. Export controls · compute thresholds and reporting · cloud KYC · HEMs · siting
  • Development process. RSPs · safety cases · licensing · weight security · audits · whistleblower protection
  • Artifact. Open-weight policy · structured access
  • Deployment. Agent governance · provenance · sectoral rules · incident reporting · liability · insurance
  • Ecosystem and international. AISIs · summits · UN track · treaties and verification · MAIM · moratorium · d/acc · windfall · public compute
  • Meta. Preemption and venue · lab structure · natsec-led approaches

Modulating parameters, 12 world-model variables that reweight every cell. Timelines · takeoff shape · polarity · alignment-difficulty distribution · offense-defense balance per domain · diffusion rate · compute concentration · warning-shot legibility · institutional response capacity · verification feasibility · delegation depth · labor-substitution rate

Three seeds name interventions that are not yet columns: control protocols, alignment research, and resilience or institutional design. Treat them as candidate columns; the table keeps each seed's own name.

The seven live debates

Each is a fight about one or two cells, and under each cell sits one parameter.

  • Halt or continue with safeguards. The fast-takeoff takeover × moratorium cell; the parameter is P(alignment intractable on current science).
  • Open weights. The misuse-bio × open-weight release cell; the parameter is irreversibility times who diffuses first.
  • Control or alignment. The scheming × control protocols cell; the parameter is the width of the useful-but-catchable window.
  • Evals sufficiency. The scheming × evals-gated commitments cell; the parameter is whether danger is measurable pre-deployment.
  • China. The great-power war × national project cell, against the same row's MAIM cell; the parameter is the rival-perception model, lock-in against preemption.
  • AGI is far. The structural drift × frontier gating cell; the parameter is whether task-horizon extrapolation holds.
  • Safety-washing. The safety-tax collapse × self-set thresholds cell; the parameter is who sets the thresholds and who runs the evals.

How to work it

  • v0 first, from priors. Fill the roughly 20 cells you think you already know before opening anything. EA-fluent readers reliably find their signs confident and their conditionals empty.
  • Update at phase gates, 30 minutes. What did this phase change, and which cells got evidence. W22 revises the decentralized-training cell, W28 revises capability-tracks-compute, W40 revises every cell that assumes danger is measurable pre-deployment.
  • The standing drill. For each threat model, name the policy it uniquely motivates and the policy at least one other threat model actively disfavors. If a model motivates nothing unique, either it collapses into a neighbor or it is not yet stated sharply enough.
  • Working copy. matrix/matrix.md in the repo, which carries the crux and a dated credence per cell. This page is the published cut.
