Worked exemplars

Six short examples of the artifact formats the weeks ask for, each at imitable length.

Formats are defined elsewhere: the claim ladder and the memo bar in How to work, the ledger row in C1, the dossier in dossiers/README.md in the repo, the eval card in W40.

Bracketed items are placeholders you fill at run time. Credences here are the writer's own numbers, dated, and carry no evidentiary weight. Every other figure traces to the week that assigns the source, or to the source register.

1. A graded claim

The sentence, as it travels. Agent task horizons are doubling roughly every seven months. It comes from METR, it is carried in the International AI Safety Report 2026, and from there it reaches briefings.

Measurement. On METR's 170-task suite (HCAST 97, RE-Bench 7, SWAA 66), with human completion times from 800-plus baselines, the fitted 50%-success horizon doubles every 207 days, 95% bootstrap CI 166–240 (Kwa et al., arXiv:2503.14499, v4 10 Jul 2026).

Scope. That suite. Clean software tasks. METR's scaffolds. Models 2019–2025. 50% success, which is a coin flip; measured horizons shrink 4–6× at 80%.

Inference, one rung up. "Frontier agents are getting better at long tasks in general." Needs the suite to stand in for work outside software. METR's own FAQ flags external validity on the same page as the chart.

Forecast, two rungs up. "A 40-hour horizon by [year]." Adds trend continuation. The bootstrap CI covers how the fit wobbles on METR's data. It does not cover trend breaks, benchmark decay, or suite contamination.

Assurance. None available. The index is human completion time, not agent runtime, so the number cannot certify that an agent runs unattended for any period.

Recommendation. Any rule built on it adds values, alternatives, authority, and cost. The measurement contains none of the four.

What it cannot support: a deployment gate, an unattended-runtime claim, a rule written at 80% reliability, or a demand forecast of any kind.

Why this passes. The measurement is stated with its instrument and its interval, and every crossing above it is labeled as a crossing rather than absorbed into the sentence.

3. A dossier extract

Threat model: misuse, biological weapons (the dossier is dossiers/misuse-biological-weapons.md in the repo, seeded from depth_threatmodels.json). Pathway plus two cruxes; the file also wants the strongest critique at full strength and one falsifiable 12-month prediction.

Pathway, my words and my probabilities. The historical constraint on a small group reaching a pandemic-capable agent has been tacit knowledge: protocol design, failure diagnosis, agent selection, and wet-lab troubleshooting that no text transfers. Frontier models compress the first three and are starting to touch the fourth through biological design tools. The path to catastrophe runs through a deliberate actor with a small budget and no institutional lab program, and the model is working as instructed the whole way. That makes it a proliferation failure, so the policy stack it implicates is synthesis screening, KYC, and access tiering rather than control or interpretability. I weight this family below structural drift, because I put most of the kill chain's difficulty downstream of information. Crux 1 is where that weighting lives.

Crux 1. Where the binding constraint sits. Information and troubleshooting, which AI addresses, or physical acquisition, somatic tacit skill, and scale-up, which it does not.

  • p = 0.3 that the binding constraint is AI-addressable (2026-08-01).
  • Moves me up: a red-team study scoring execution rather than planning documents that finds a viability gap above its own detection floor. RAND's 2024 design could not have seen one, at 12 cells and roughly 1.0 point detectable.
  • Moves me down: a documented interdiction at procurement or synthesis screening that caught an attempt the information stage did not.

Crux 2. Do public biorisk benchmark scores predict end-to-end capability?

  • p = 0.15 that a validated link is published by 2027-08-01 (2026-08-01).
  • Moves me: a study relating wet-lab troubleshooting scores to an end-to-end outcome measure, with the expert baseline stated. The Virology Capabilities Test anchors the input side at 22.1% for expert virologists inside their own sub-areas. The outcome side is the missing half.
  • Ho and Berg conclude only that evaluations are insufficient to rule risk out. That is a case for precaution, not positive evidence of uplift, and it is usually quoted as the second thing.

Why this passes. Each crux is a belief whose flip changes a recommendation, each credence carries a date, and each observable names a direction and a rough size. A crux with no named observable is a topic.

4. An eval card

Fields per W40. The suite is fictional. The arithmetic is not.

Construct. Whether the model, asked to summarize a capability claim, states the scope limits the source itself attaches to it. Decision served: whether a two-person policy team can triage incoming capability claims without a human rereading each source. Stack level: model plus prompt, no tools. Deviations from the W36 spec: none.

Population. 24 hand-written items, each pairing a real capability sentence with the caveats its own source states. Claims to stand in for capability claims arriving in a policy inbox. Does not cover multi-document claims, non-English sources, or claims whose source states no caveat.

Task set. scope-triage-v0.3, committed [date], [hash].

Elicitation. Temperature 1.0, single turn, no tools, 800-token cap, 3 samples per item, seeds logged. Any response hitting the cap is scored as-is and flagged.

Harness. Inspect [version], model [id and version string], no sandbox.

Scoring. Model-graded on three binary criteria: names a caveat the source states; names no caveat the source does not state; does not raise the claim's rung. Grader [model id], prompt verbatim in the log. Hand-check: 30 transcripts regraded by me, agreement [n/30].

Uncertainty. Two runs at identical configuration scored 58% and 62%, a 4-point spread. Item-sampling standard error at n = 24 and p ≈ 0.6 is √(0.6 × 0.4 / 24) ≈ 10 points. The second swamps the first. This suite supports "the model does this most of the time" and cannot support a 10-point model-versus-model comparison.

Validity threats.

threatdetectorresult

Decision rule. Below 50% on the stricter grader, triage for that source type returns to a human. The team lead acts. The threshold reads the run mean, not a single run, and is rechecked whenever the grader or the model version changes.

Why this passes. A second run at the same configuration lands inside the stated spread, and the two uncertainties are reported separately instead of averaged into one error bar.

5. A memo opening

First 150 words of a two-page BLUF memo. Bar per How to work: recommendation up front, a named principal who can act, every strong sentence surviving a source-scope-uncertainty audit. Material from R11.

To [name], [org] · From [you] · Date [date] · Re Large-load cost allocation before the [state] commission Recommendation. File in the commission's large-load tariff proceeding and skip FERC's RM26-4 docket this quarter. The decision that sets who pays for the [project] campus is retail rate design, and retail rates sit with the state under Federal Power Act section 201(b). RM26-4 is testing whether large-load interconnection is federal business at all. Even a favorable outcome there leaves rate design with your commission. Options. (1) State tariff intervention: a large-load class with minimum-take terms, collateral, and exit fees, priced on cost causation. Cheapest, nearest deadline, most leverage. (2) RM26-4 comments only: national visibility, no effect on this project's rates. (3) Both, which splits two staff across two clocks and files neither well. Below: the authority map with appeal paths, the cost-causation argument, and the two load numbers the commission will ask you for.

Why this passes.

  • The first sentence is the ask, with an instrument and a docket.
  • The load-bearing jurisdictional claim cites the statute section it rests on.
  • The dismissed option is dismissed on a stated reason rather than omitted.
  • Option 3 is named and priced, so the principal can see what hedging costs.

6. The same memo, failing

Same brief, second attempt. Competent prose about real things, and it fails.

Re AI data centers and the grid Data center demand is growing rapidly. Utilities in the region report a combined [N] GW of pending large-load requests, up sharply from last year, and interconnection queues continue to lengthen. FERC is considering large-load interconnection in Docket RM26-4. Meanwhile agent task horizons are doubling every seven months, so compute demand will keep climbing. State policymakers should take these pressures seriously and consider whether current tariff structures remain appropriate. This memo reviews the background, surveys the relevant authorities, and offers considerations for further study.

Margin notes.

  1. No ask, no actor. "Should take seriously" and "consider whether" name no instrument, no docket, and nobody with authority. A principal cannot act on page one.
  2. Queue totals summed. One project shops several utilities and holds positions in more than one queue, so a regional sum double-counts by construction. The honest sentence gives a range whose floor is zero (R11).
  3. "Up sharply from last year" has no source, no baseline year, and no ledger row. Retrofitting citations onto finished prose is how numbers get invented.
  4. A fitted parameter crossed into a demand forecast. The 207-day doubling is measured on a software-task suite at 50% success. Nothing in it is about electricity. Two ladder rungs are crossed inside one clause (W32, W38).
  5. Jurisdiction named but not resolved. Citing RM26-4 without saying whether this decision is federal or retail leaves the reader unable to tell who the memo is for.
  6. The roadmap paragraph. It tells the principal what they are about to read instead of what to do. Cutting it costs nothing.

Repair order. Fix 1 first. Writing the ask forces 2, 4, and 5 into the open.
