## Design Context

### Users
The site is for readers of Andy Masley's blog and broader body of work. They are usually arriving to read essays, browse archives, follow references, explore teaching materials, or interact with data visualizations tied to the writing. The interface should help them move through a body of thought without friction or visual clutter.

### Brand Personality
Minimalist, tasteful, Borges.

The site should feel calm, intelligent, literary, and intentional. It should suggest a serious but curious writer's archive rather than a startup product, a content farm, or an AI-generated portfolio.

### Aesthetic Direction
Editorial and bookish rather than app-like. Use restraint, hierarchy, rhythm, and typography to carry the design. Favor warm neutrals, clean rules, sharp edges where appropriate, and a measured accent palette. Avoid sloppy composition, generic rounded-card UI, glossy gradients, loud metric blocks, empty decorative chrome, and other obvious AI-slop tropes.

Support both light mode and dark mode, with each feeling deliberate rather than merely inverted.

### Design Principles
1. Typography over chrome. Let type, spacing, and alignment do more of the work than containers, pills, or decorative effects.
2. Quiet confidence. Keep the interface restrained and legible; use emphasis sparingly so it means something when it appears.
3. Literary navigation. Browsing should feel like moving through a thoughtful archive, not operating a dashboard.
4. Consistent restraint. Avoid sloppy or generic UI gestures anywhere on the site, including utilities, overlays, and interactive tools.
5. Distinct but not flashy. The site should feel authored and memorable without becoming loud, gimmicky, or overdesigned.

### Quality Bar
Flagship personal site. Changes should feel considered and durable, not trendy, clever, or merely cleaned up.
